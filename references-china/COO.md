## What a COO Actually Is (and Why It Matters for Selling)

The COO is the **execution engine** of the enterprise. Where the CEO sets direction and the CFO allocates capital, the COO converts strategy into repeatable, scalable operational reality. In China, this role carries additional weight because:

1. **Manufacturing density** — China remains the world's factory. Operational excellence is not a support function; it IS the business for many firms.
2. **Policy alignment** — Government initiatives (中国制造2025 successor programs, 智能制造示范工厂) create both incentives and mandates that the COO must navigate.
3. **Cost pressure** — 降本增效 (reduce costs, increase efficiency) is the universal mantra in the current economic environment. Every board meeting asks the COO: "Where is the next 5% margin?"
4. **Supply chain trauma** — Post-COVID disruptions created an existential awareness of supply chain fragility. Resilience is no longer optional.
5. **出海 complexity** — For companies expanding globally, the COO runs dual operations: China-based production AND global distribution/fulfillment.

**Key insight for sellers:** The COO buys solutions that produce *measurable operational outcomes* — throughput, cost per unit, cycle time, uptime, defect rate. Abstract "digital transformation" pitches fail. Concrete "reduce your order-to-delivery from 14 days to 7 days" pitches win.

## §1 · Role Definition

### 1.1 Six COO Archetypes in China

Not all COOs are the same. The archetype determines what they care about, what language resonates, and what proof points matter.

| # | Archetype | Description | China Example Pattern |
|---|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| 1 | **The Factory Optimizer** (生产型) | Runs manufacturing operations; obsessed with OEE, yield, throughput | 理想汽车 gigafactory ramp; Haier 灯塔工厂 smart manufacturing |
| 2 | **The Supply Chain Architect** (供应链型) | Designs and manages supplier networks, procurement, logistics | Shein 小单快反 model with 1000s of suppliers; Anker global warehouse network |
| 3 | **The Platform Scaler** (平台型) | Manages operations at massive digital scale; reliability, moderation, SLA | ByteDance operating 2B+ users; 港交所 99.99% uptime |
| 4 | **The Fulfillment Commander** (履约型) | Owns last-mile delivery, customer experience in logistics | JD Logistics; SF Express; Meituan instant delivery |
| 5 | **The 出海 Bridge** (出海型) | Dual-mode: China production + global distribution and compliance | Anker DTC + Amazon channel ops; Shein global fulfillment |
| 6 | **The Turnaround Operator** (变革型) | Brought in to fix broken operations; aggressive timeline, ruthless prioritization | Cathay Pacific post-COVID recovery; restructuring mandates |

| Signal | Likely Archetype |
|--------|-----------------|
| Talks about OEE, yield rate, production lines | Factory Optimizer |
| Mentions supplier count, MOQ, lead time | Supply Chain Architect |
| Discusses uptime, SLA, concurrent users, moderation | Platform Scaler |
| Focuses on delivery time, last-mile cost, order accuracy | Fulfillment Commander |
| References multiple countries, customs, localization | 出海 Bridge |

### 1.3 Three Time Horizons the COO Manages Simultaneously

| Horizon | Timeframe | Focus | Typical China Pressure |
|---------|-----------|-------|----------------------|
| **H1 — Keep the lights on** | Today–90 days | Daily ops, incident response, SLA delivery | 安全生产 compliance, quality incidents, supply disruptions |
| **H2 — Optimize the machine** | 3–18 months | Process improvement, cost reduction, capacity planning | 降本增效 targets from board; 灯塔工厂 certification pursuits |
| **H3 — Build next-gen operations** | 18–36 months | New operating models, automation, new market ops infrastructure | 智能制造 roadmap; 出海 operational buildout |

**Key insight:** Most vendors pitch H3 vision. COOs live in H1 pain. Bridge the gap by showing how your H3 solution delivers H1 relief within 90 days.

### 1.4 The Four-Way Pull

Every COO in China is pulled in four directions simultaneously:

```
                    ┌─────────────┐
                    │   QUALITY   │
                    │  (质量第一)  │
                    └──────┬──────┘
                           │
         ┌─────────────────┼─────────────────┐
         │                 │                 │
    ┌────┴────┐            │           ┌────┴────┐
    │  COST   │            │           │  SPEED  │
    │(降本增效)│◄───────────┼──────────►│(快速响应)│
    └─────────┘            │           └─────────┘
                           │
                    ┌──────┴──────┐
                    │ COMPLIANCE  │
                    │ (合规安全)   │
                    └─────────────┘
```

- **Cost vs. Speed** — Cheaper materials slow production; faster delivery costs more
- **Quality vs. Cost** — Higher quality standards increase unit cost
- **Speed vs. Compliance** — Moving fast may skip safety/environmental checks
- **Compliance vs. Speed** — Regulatory requirements add process steps

The COO's job is to find the Pareto frontier across all four. Solutions that improve one dimension WITHOUT degrading others are irresistible.

## §2 · Strategic Priorities (Current Cycle: 2024–2027)

### Priority 1: 降本增效 — Cost Reduction Without Capability Loss

The #1 mandate. In the current macro environment (slower growth, margin compression, investor pressure), every COO has explicit cost targets.

- **What it means in practice:** 5–15% annual cost reduction targets across operations
- **How they achieve it:** Automation, supplier consolidation, process elimination, energy efficiency
- **China nuance:** Labor costs rising 8–12% annually in coastal cities; automation ROI increasingly favorable
- **Proof point pattern:** "Haier's COSMOPlat reduced manufacturing cost per unit by 20% while increasing customization capability"

### Priority 2: 供应链韧性 — Supply Chain Resilience

Post-COVID, post-geopolitical-tension supply chain design. Not just efficiency — survivability.

- **Dual-sourcing mandates:** No single supplier >30% of critical components
- **Inventory strategy shift:** From JIT (just-in-time) to JIC (just-in-case) for critical materials
- **Geographic diversification:** China+1 manufacturing; ASEAN backup capacity
- **Digital visibility:** Real-time supply chain visibility across all tiers
- **China nuance:** 自主可控 (self-reliance) for critical technology components; semiconductor supply chain is board-level topic

### Priority 3: 智能制造 — Smart Manufacturing / Industry 4.0

Government-incentivized, board-demanded modernization of production operations.

- **灯塔工厂 (Lighthouse Factory):** WEF-designated factories demonstrating 4IR excellence; China has the most globally
- **Digital twin deployment:** Virtual models of production lines for optimization
- **Predictive maintenance:** AI-driven equipment maintenance reducing downtime
- **China nuance:** Government subsidies available; provincial competition for 智能制造示范 designations creates urgency
- **Proof point pattern:** "理想汽车's smart factory achieved 40-second takt time through digital twin optimization"

### Priority 4: 柔性供应链 — Flexible/Agile Supply Chain

The ability to respond to demand volatility without massive inventory or capacity buffers.

- **小单快反 model:** Small-batch, quick-response production (Shein produces 100-piece test runs, scales winners)
- **Modular production:** Reconfigurable lines that switch products in hours, not weeks
- **Demand sensing:** AI-driven demand forecasting integrated with production scheduling
- **China nuance:** Consumer market volatility (直播电商 livestream spikes) requires extreme agility

### Priority 5: 出海运营 — Global Operations Buildout

For companies expanding internationally, building operations infrastructure abroad.

- **Overseas warehouse network:** Fulfillment centers in target markets (US, EU, SEA)
- **Cross-border logistics optimization:** Customs, duties, routing
- **Local compliance:** Each market's labor, safety, environmental regulations
- **Proof point pattern:** "Anker operates 30+ overseas warehouses with 2-day delivery capability in core markets"

### Priority 6: 安全生产与合规 — Production Safety and Compliance

Non-negotiable regulatory requirements with severe penalties for failure.

- **安全生产法** compliance: Production safety law with personal criminal liability for executives
- **Environmental standards:** Carbon reporting, emissions targets, ESG disclosure requirements
- **Labor compliance:** Working hour limits, social insurance, workplace safety
- **Data compliance:** For platform operators — data localization, content moderation requirements
- **China nuance:** Local government inspections intensifying; one incident can shut down operations

### Priority 7: 人效提升 — Workforce Productivity

Getting more output per person without simply adding headcount.

- **Automation of repetitive tasks:** RPA, physical robotics, AI-assisted decisions
- **Skill upgrading:** Training existing workforce for higher-value activities
- **Gig/flexible workforce models:** Scaling labor up/down with demand
- **China nuance:** Demographic shift (aging workforce) makes automation not optional but necessary

### Priority 8: 数据驱动运营 — Data-Driven Operations

Moving from experience-based to data-based operational decision-making.

- **Operational data platform:** Unified view across production, supply chain, logistics
- **Real-time dashboards:** From daily reports to real-time visibility
- **AI/ML for optimization:** Routing, scheduling, quality prediction, demand planning
- **China nuance:** Data infrastructure often fragmented across legacy systems; integration is the real challenge

### Priority 9: 可持续运营 — Sustainable Operations

ESG-driven operational transformation, increasingly mandated by regulation and customers.

- **Carbon neutrality roadmaps:** Scope 1, 2, 3 emissions tracking and reduction
- **Circular economy:** Waste reduction, material recycling, product lifecycle
- **Green supply chain:** Supplier ESG scoring, sustainable sourcing requirements
- **China nuance:** 双碳目标 (dual carbon goals) create regulatory pressure; green factory certifications (绿色工厂) as competitive advantage

## §4 · Pain Points

### Pain Point 1: Legacy System Fragmentation (系统孤岛)

**The problem:** Operations run on 5–15 disconnected systems — ERP, MES, WMS, TMS, QMS — that don't talk to each other. Data lives in silos.

- **Consequence:** No single source of truth; decisions based on stale data; reconciliation consumes analyst time
- **Emotional weight:** Frustration; the COO KNOWS better data exists but can't access it fast enough
- **China nuance:** Many systems are localized versions (用友, 金蝶) that integrate poorly with global tools; legacy factory systems use proprietary protocols
- **What they say:** "我们有数据，但数据不通" (We have data, but data doesn't flow)

### Pain Point 2: Cost Pressure Without Investment Budget (既要降本又不给预算)

**The problem:** Board demands 10% cost reduction but won't approve the CapEx needed for automation/modernization.

- **Consequence:** COO caught between targets and resources; forced into incremental optimization instead of step-change improvement
- **Emotional weight:** Trapped; politically exposed if targets missed
- **China nuance:** In current macro environment, CFOs are extremely conservative on CapEx; COO must build ROI cases that show payback in <12 months
- **What they say:** "先用最小投入做出效果来" (Show results with minimal investment first)

### Pain Point 3: Talent Gap in Digital Operations (数字化人才缺口)

**The problem:** Smart manufacturing and data-driven operations require skills the existing workforce doesn't have. Digital talent prefers internet companies over manufacturing.

- **Consequence:** Automation projects stall; tools deployed but underutilized; reliance on vendors for ongoing operation
- **Emotional weight:** Anxiety about organizational capability; fear of vendor dependency
- **China nuance:** Competition for digital talent is fierce; 智能制造 talent commands 50–100% premium; retention is a constant battle
- **What they say:** "工具买了，但没人会用" (We bought the tools but nobody knows how to use them)

### Pain Point 4: Supply Chain Visibility Gap (供应链可视性不足)

**The problem:** Tier-1 suppliers are somewhat visible; Tier-2 and below are opaque. Disruptions emerge without warning.

- **Consequence:** Reactive crisis management; excess safety stock (tying up cash); inability to make proactive sourcing decisions
- **Emotional weight:** Vulnerability; PTSD from COVID-era disruptions
- **China nuance:** Many suppliers are SMEs with minimal digital infrastructure; data sharing requires relationship (关系) not just contracts
- **What they say:** "去年缺芯的时候，我们到第三周才知道问题在哪" (During the chip shortage, it took us three weeks to identify where the problem was)

### Pain Point 5: Scaling Operations for 出海 (海外运营体系搭建)

**The problem:** Operations built for China don't transplant directly overseas. Different regulations, labor markets, logistics infrastructure, customer expectations.

- **Consequence:** High cost of overseas operations; quality/speed gap vs. China operations; compliance risks
- **Emotional weight:** Overwhelm; too many unknowns; fear of costly mistakes
- **China nuance:** 出海 is strategic mandate from board/investors; COO expected to "figure it out" with limited overseas experience
- **What they say:** "国内三天能搞定的事，到了海外要三周" (What takes 3 days domestically takes 3 weeks overseas)

### Pain Point 6: Balancing Speed and Compliance (速度与合规的平衡)

**The problem:** Business demands rapid execution; regulatory environment demands thorough compliance. These conflict daily.

- **Consequence:** Either compliance corners are cut (risk) or speed suffers (competitive disadvantage)
- **Emotional weight:** Personal liability fear (安全生产法 holds executives criminally responsible); career risk
- **China nuance:** Regulatory enforcement is inconsistent but consequences are severe when enforced; data security law (数据安全法) adds new compliance layer for platform operators
- **What they say:** "合规是底线，但业务不等人" (Compliance is the floor, but business won't wait)

## §5 · Objection Patterns

### Objection 1: "我们现在不是上系统的时候" (Now is not the time for new systems)

**Translation:** We're too busy fighting fires to implement something new.

**What's really happening:** Fear of disruption to already-stressed operations; implementation fatigue from past projects.

**Counter-strategy:**
- Acknowledge operational pressure explicitly
- Propose phased approach: "先在一条线试" (pilot on one line first)
- Show implementation timeline that doesn't disrupt current production
- Reference peers who implemented during similar pressure periods

### Objection 2: "ROI算不过来" (The ROI doesn't pencil out)

**Translation:** Payback period is too long or benefits too uncertain.

**What's really happening:** CFO has set <12-month payback requirement; COO needs ammunition for internal business case.

**Counter-strategy:**
- Provide ROI calculator with their actual operational data
- Show quick-win use cases that deliver value in <90 days
- Offer gain-sharing or outcome-based pricing models
- Provide peer benchmarks: "同行业企业6个月回本" (Industry peers see payback in 6 months)

### Objection 3: "我们团队用不了这么复杂的东西" (Our team can't handle something this complex)

**Translation:** Capability gap concern; fear of shelfware.

**What's really happening:** Past implementations failed due to user adoption; legitimate concern about workforce readiness.

**Counter-strategy:**
- Emphasize simplicity of user interface; show demo to frontline users, not just executives
- Offer embedded customer success / implementation support
- Reference similar-profile companies (same industry, same team capability) who succeeded
- Propose train-the-trainer model with certification

### Objection 4: "我们自己能做" (We can build this ourselves)

**Translation:** Internal development team can replicate this; why pay external vendor?

**What's really happening:** CTO/IT team protecting territory; genuine belief in internal capability; cost sensitivity.

**Counter-strategy:**
- Acknowledge internal capability respectfully
- Quantify opportunity cost: engineer-months diverted from core product/operations
- Highlight maintenance burden: "建系统容易，养系统难" (Building is easy; maintaining is hard)
- Position as "buy the foundation, customize the top": platform + configuration

### Objection 5: "数据安全怎么保证" (How do you guarantee data security?)

**Translation:** Operational data is sensitive; concerns about data leaving the organization.

**What's really happening:** Genuine security concern amplified by regulatory environment (数据安全法, 个人信息保护法); IT/security team veto power.

**Counter-strategy:**
- Lead with deployment options: on-premise, private cloud, hybrid
- Show certifications: 等保三级, ISO 27001, SOC 2
- Provide data flow diagrams showing what data goes where
- Offer security review / penetration test before deployment
- Reference government/SOE customers if applicable (social proof of compliance)

### Objection 6: "先等等看，明年再说" (Let's wait and see, revisit next year)

**Translation:** Deferral; not saying no, but not saying yes.

**What's really happening:** Competing priorities; budget cycle misalignment; decision fatigue; insufficient urgency.

**Counter-strategy:**
- Quantify cost of delay: "每推迟一个月，损失XX万" (Each month of delay costs XX)
- Create urgency through competitive intelligence: "竞品已经在用了" (Competitors are already using this)
- Align with budget cycle: propose planning now for next fiscal year start
- Offer low-commitment entry: POC, pilot, or assessment

### Objection 7: "跟我们现有系统能对接吗" (Can this integrate with our existing systems?)

**Translation:** Integration complexity is the real concern; past integration nightmares.

**What's really happening:** Technical debt is massive; every new system adds complexity; IT team is stretched.

**Counter-strategy:**
- Come prepared with specific integration architecture for their stack (用友/金蝶/SAP)
- Show existing connectors and API documentation
- Offer integration as part of implementation scope (not customer's problem)
- Reference successful integrations with same systems at peer companies

### Objection 8: "你们懂我们这个行业吗" (Do you understand our industry?)

**Translation:** Skepticism about domain expertise; fear of generic solutions applied naively.

**What's really happening:** COOs are deeply operational — they smell non-practitioners immediately; need confidence that vendor understands their specific operational reality.

**Counter-strategy:**
- Lead with industry-specific metrics and language (not generic "digital transformation")
- Bring domain expert / implementation consultant to the meeting
- Show case studies from same industry, same operational model
- Acknowledge what's unique about their operation; don't pretend one-size-fits-all

## §6 · Buying Dynamics

### 6.1 How the COO Buys

| Dimension | Behavior |
|-----------|----------|
| **Decision style** | Data-driven + operational intuition; wants proof, not promises |
| **Budget authority** | Typically owns OpEx budget for operations; CapEx requires CFO/CEO co-sign |
| **Typical deal size owned** | ¥500K–¥10M independently; >¥10M involves CEO/CFO |
| **Evaluation method** | Pilot/POC strongly preferred; "先试再买" (try before buying) |
| **Timeline** | Wants fast results (90-day value); patient on full rollout if pilot proves value |
| **Influencers** | Production managers, supply chain directors, IT/CTO (integration), CFO (budget) |
| **Blockers** | IT (security/integration concerns), CFO (cost), production teams (change resistance) |

### 6.2 Decision Process (Typical 6–12 Month Cycle)

```
Discovery          →  Internal Alignment  →  Pilot/POC       →  Expansion
(1-2 months)          (1-3 months)           (2-4 months)       (ongoing)
                                                                  
- Problem acute       - Build business case  - Single line/     - Roll to all
- Vendor research     - CFO budget approval    site/process       lines/sites
- Peer references     - IT security review   - Prove ROI        - Enterprise
- Initial demos       - Stakeholder buy-in   - User adoption      contract
```

### 6.3 What Makes Them Say Yes

| Factor | Weight | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Proven peer results** | Very High | Same industry, same scale, verifiable |
| **Fast time-to-value** | Very High | Value within 90 days of deployment |
| **Low implementation risk** | High | Phased approach, doesn't disrupt current ops |
| **Integration ease** | High | Works with existing systems without rip-and-replace |
| **Clear ROI model** | High | Specific, auditable numbers they can take to CFO |
| **Vendor operational depth** | Medium-High | Vendor must understand their operations |
| **Total cost of ownership** | Medium | Not just license; implementation + maintenance + training |

### 6.4 What Makes Them Say No

- Vendor can't articulate specific operational impact in their context
- Implementation timeline conflicts with production peaks (双十一, 春节 pre-season)
- Requires significant IT resources they don't have
- No reference customer in their industry/scale
- Previous negative experience with same vendor or similar solution category
- Solution requires operational process change without clear benefit

### 6.5 Internal Champions and Blockers

| Role | Likely Stance | How to Engage |

| **Production Director** (生产总监) | Champion IF solves daily pain | Show frontline UX; involve early |

## §7 · Discovery Questions

### 7.1 Opening Questions (Establish Context)

1. "您目前运营体系最大的瓶颈在哪里？" (Where is the biggest bottleneck in your current operational system?)
2. "今年董事会给运营团队的核心目标是什么？" (What are the board's core targets for the operations team this year?)
3. "您的运营团队规模和组织架构是怎样的？" (What's the size and structure of your operations organization?)
4. "目前用哪些核心系统在管理运营？" (What core systems are you currently using to manage operations?)

### 7.2 Pain-Discovery Questions (Deepen Understanding)

5. "上一次重大运营中断是什么时候？根因是什么？" (When was the last major operational disruption? What was the root cause?)
6. "从接到订单到交付，中间有哪些环节您觉得可以压缩？" (In your order-to-delivery process, which steps do you feel could be compressed?)
7. "供应链可视性做到了第几层？" (How many tiers of supply chain visibility do you currently have?)
8. "降本目标具体是怎么分解的？哪个环节压力最大？" (How are your cost reduction targets broken down? Where is the most pressure?)

### 7.3 Impact-Quantification Questions (Build Business Case)

9. "一个小时的产线停机，对你们意味着多少损失？" (What does one hour of production line downtime cost you?)
10. "目前的良品率是多少？每提升一个百分点意味着什么？" (What's your current yield rate? What does each percentage point of improvement mean?)
11. "库存周转如果能快一圈，对现金流的影响有多大？" (If inventory turns improved by one cycle, what's the cash flow impact?)
12. "如果交付周期能缩短30%，对客户满意度和复购有什么影响？" (If delivery cycle shortened by 30%, what's the impact on customer satisfaction and repeat purchases?)

### 7.4 Decision-Process Questions (Navigate the Sale)

13. "这类项目的决策流程通常是怎样的？" (What's the typical decision process for this type of project?)
14. "除了您之外，还有谁需要参与评估？" (Besides you, who else needs to be involved in the evaluation?)
15. "预算是已经立项了还是需要单独申请？" (Is budget already allocated or does it need separate approval?)
16. "如果我们做一个小范围试点，您倾向于从哪个环节开始？" (If we did a limited pilot, which area would you prefer to start with?)

### 7.5 Competitive/Status-Quo Questions (Understand Alternatives)

17. "目前有在评估其他方案吗？" (Are you currently evaluating other solutions?)
18. "之前有没有尝试过类似的项目？结果如何？" (Have you tried similar projects before? What were the results?)
19. "如果不做改变，按现在的模式再跑一年会怎样？" (If nothing changes and you run current operations for another year, what happens?)

## §8 · Communication Style

### 8.1 How COOs Talk

| Dimension | Characteristic |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Language register** | Direct, operational, metric-laden; less abstract than CEO, less financial than CFO |
| **Preferred format** | Dashboards, process flows, before/after comparisons; NOT slide decks with vision statements |
| **Meeting style** | Short, structured, action-oriented; wants decisions not discussions |
| **Time orientation** | Present-focused with execution milestones; "什么时候能上线" (when can this go live?) |
| **Trust signals** | Specificity, operational knowledge, peer references, realistic timelines |
| **Distrust signals** | Vagueness, over-promising, lack of domain knowledge, "trust me" without data |

### 8.2 Language Patterns — What They Say vs. What They Mean

| What They Say | What They Mean |
|---------------|----------------|
| "这个方案理论上不错" (This solution is good in theory) | I don't believe it works in practice; show me proof |
| "我们先内部讨论一下" (Let us discuss internally first) | You haven't convinced me; I need time/cover |
| "能不能先做个小试点" (Can we do a small pilot first?) | I'm interested but need to de-risk; this is a buying signal |
| "我们的情况比较特殊" (Our situation is quite unique) | You haven't demonstrated you understand our business |
| "时间节点能保证吗" (Can you guarantee the timeline?) | I've been burned by late deliveries before; this is a real concern |
| "落地的时候谁来负责" (Who's responsible during implementation?) | I need a named person, not a "team"; accountability matters |
| "我跟IT那边确认一下" (Let me check with IT) | IT may be a blocker; there's a technical concern I can't evaluate myself |

### 8.3 Communication Preferences by Archetype

| Archetype | Preferred Proof | Meeting Format | Key Phrase |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|------------|
| Factory Optimizer | OEE improvement data; factory visit | On-site at their factory | "帮我把OEE从75提到85" |
| Supply Chain Architect | Visibility demo; disruption scenarios | Virtual + data room | "让我看到第三层供应商" |
| Platform Scaler | Uptime data; load test results | Technical deep-dive | "能扛住峰值吗" |
| Fulfillment Commander | Delivery time reduction; cost per order | Dashboard walkthrough | "一单成本降到多少" |
| 出海 Bridge | Multi-market case study; compliance matrix | Cross-timezone call | "海外能复制国内的效率吗" |
| Turnaround Operator | Speed of implementation; quick wins | Tight 30-min executive brief | "三个月能见效吗" |

### 8.4 Email/Message Cadence

- **Response time expectation:** COOs are fast responders when interested (within hours, not days)
- **Preferred communication:** WeChat for ongoing; email for formal proposals; phone for urgent
- **Meeting length:** 30–45 minutes max for initial meetings; COOs are time-poor
- **Follow-up format:** One-page summary with action items, timeline, next steps — NOT lengthy decks
- **Winning format:** "三张图讲清楚" (Explain it in three diagrams) — process flow, before/after metrics, implementation timeline

### 8.5 Cultural Nuances for China COOs

| Nuance | Implication |
|--------|-------------|
| **Face (面子)** | Never imply their current operations are "bad"; frame as "good to great" |
| **Relationship (关系)** | Peer introductions are extremely powerful; industry associations matter |
| **Hierarchy** | Even if COO is interested, may defer to CEO; understand reporting dynamics |
| **Pragmatism** | Chinese COOs are intensely practical; philosophical pitches fail |
| **Speed expectation** | "互联网速度" (internet speed) — expect vendor responsiveness matching their own pace |
| **Group decision** | Rarely decide alone; consensus-building is part of the process; be patient |
| **Government context** | Mentioning alignment with government priorities (智能制造, 双碳) can unlock budget |

### 8.6 Presentation Structure That Works

```
1. 行业洞察 (Industry insight)          — Show you understand their world    [2 min]
2. 同行案例 (Peer case study)           — Credibility through proof          [5 min]  
3. 方案概述 (Solution overview)          — Specific to their operational context [5 min]
4. 落地路径 (Implementation roadmap)     — Phased, realistic, low-risk         [5 min]
5. 投入产出 (ROI model)                 — Numbers they can take to CFO        [3 min]
6. 下一步 (Next steps)                  — Specific ask: pilot scope, timeline  [2 min]
```

**Total: 22 minutes** — leaves time for Q&A in a 30-minute slot.
