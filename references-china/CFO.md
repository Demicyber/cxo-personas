## What a CFO Actually Is — China Context

The CFO role in China is **not equivalent** to its Western counterpart. Understanding the structural differences is prerequisite to any engagement:

### 民企 (Private Enterprise) CFO
In most Chinese private companies, the CFO is functionally a **高级出纳** (senior cashier) — they execute the founder's financial will rather than setting strategy. The founder controls capital allocation, often maintains personal relationships with banks, and may keep a parallel set of books. The CFO's power peaks during IPO preparation (when auditors and regulators demand a credible finance leader) and wanes after listing stabilizes. At companies like Anker (安克创新), the CFO manages DTC unit economics and investor communications but strategic resource allocation remains with founder 阳萌. At ByteDance, the CFO manages a $110B+ revenue machine but TikTok/抖音 resource allocation is driven by 张一鸣's long-term vision framework.

### 国企 (State-Owned Enterprise) CFO
SOE CFOs operate within the **SASAC 考核 (assessment) framework** — their KPIs are literally set by the state. They must navigate 三重一大 (三重一大事项集体决策制度: major decisions, major personnel appointments, major projects, large capital expenditures require collective decision-making). The CFO at 海康威视 must balance commercial optimization with CETC (中国电子科技集团) parent requirements, state-directed investment mandates, and transfer pricing that satisfies both SASAC assessments and minority shareholders. Their discretion is bounded; their compliance burden is immense.

### 外企 (Foreign Enterprise) CFO
China CFOs at multinationals are **regional controllers** — they report to APAC or Global CFO, execute HQ policy locally, and manage China-specific regulatory compliance (税务局, 外汇管理局, 审计). Their strategic influence is limited to China P&L optimization within global guardrails. Cathay Pacific's finance function illustrates the hybrid: HK-listed but Swire-controlled, managing aviation finance with both HKEX and mainland regulatory requirements.

### 创业公司 (Startup) CFO
At early-stage companies like 禾观 (Series A/B), the "CFO" may be a **财务经理** (Finance Manager) promoted into the title for fundraising credibility. Their entire job is burn-rate management, runway extension, and preparing materials for the next round. They live in spreadsheets showing months-of-runway, not strategic financial architecture.

### 双重上市 (Dual-Listed) CFO
Companies like 理想汽车 (LI — NASDAQ + HKEX) require a CFO who simultaneously satisfies SEC, HKEX, and mainland regulatory requirements. This is the most complex CFO role in the China ecosystem — managing three sets of disclosure requirements, multiple audit relationships, and investor bases with fundamentally different expectations. The CFO here is genuinely strategic because the complexity demands it.

### 转型期 (Transition-Period) CFO
At companies like 猎豹移动 (NYSE-listed, revenue declining), the CFO manages investor expectations during a painful pivot. Their job is buying time — maintaining listing compliance, managing cash reserves, and telling a credible forward story while the business model transforms beneath them.

### Three Time Horizons (China-Adapted)

Unlike Western CFOs who plan in quarters, China CFOs operate on three distinct but overlapping calendars:

| Horizon | Timeframe | Driving Force | Key Activities |
|---------|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Fiscal Execution** | Current FY (Jan-Dec or Apr-Mar) | Budget delivery, SASAC 年度考核 | Monthly close, cash management, cost control, 两会-aligned budgeting |
| **Audit Cycle** | 12-18 months | Annual audit + regulatory examination | Auditor management, internal control remediation, disclosure preparation |
| **Capital Window** | 18-36 months | Funding rounds, refinancing, listing milestones | IPO preparation, bond maturity management, equity story development |

**Critical timing note:** 两会 (March) sets policy tone → SASAC issues assessment targets (Q1) → Budget finalization follows (Q1-Q2). Enterprise software purchases often cluster in Q4 (use-it-or-lose-it budget) or Q2 (new budget approved). IPO-track companies have compressed, externally-driven timelines.

### The Four-Way Pull

Every China CFO is pulled in four directions simultaneously. The relative force varies by archetype:

```
                    CEO / Founder
                        ↑
                        |
                   [MANDATE]
                        |
    Auditors/     ←— CFO —→     Operations/
    Regulators    [COMPLIANCE]    Business Units
                        |
                   [CREDIBILITY]
                        |
                        ↓
                    Investors
```

| Pull Direction | What They Demand | CFO's Response |
|---------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **CEO/Founder** | Growth funding, speed, flexibility, "make it work" | Balances ambition against cash reality; says "no" diplomatically or finds creative structures |
| **Auditors/Regulators** | Clean books, proper disclosure, GAAP/IFRS compliance, 税务合规 | Maintains audit-ready state; manages auditor relationship as strategic asset |
| **Investors** | Returns, predictability, transparency, competitive multiples | Crafts narrative, manages guidance, delivers or explains variance |
| **Operations/BUs** | Budget, headcount, capex approval, speed of procurement | Gates spending while maintaining partnership; avoids being seen as "blocker" |

**Archetype variation:**
- 创始人钱袋子型: CEO pull dominates (80%); CFO is execution arm
- 资本市场型: Investor pull dominates (50%); CFO is chief storyteller
- 政策型/体制型: Regulator pull dominates (60%); CFO is compliance engine
- 出海合规型: All four pull roughly equally (25% each); CFO is coordinator
- 烧钱续命型: Investor pull dominates (70%); CFO is fundraising support
- 分权损益型: Operations pull dominates (50%); CFO is internal banker

### Priority Deep-Dives by Archetype

**创始人钱袋子型 (Founder's Wallet):**
- Priority #1 is whatever the founder says it is today
- Cash management is about funding the founder's next bet
- Tax optimization is personal (founder wealth) as much as corporate
- Government subsidies are pursued aggressively — free money for growth

**资本市场型 (Capital Markets Operator):**
- IR dominates mental bandwidth — quarterly rhythm drives everything
- IPO readiness is existential for pre-listing; post-listing it's about maintaining standards
- FX management critical for dual-listed (manage ADR premium/discount)
- 理想's CFO manages ¥144.5B revenue while burning cash on factories — story must be "investment phase, not loss-making"

**政策型/体制型 (Policy-Constrained):**
- SASAC 考核 KPIs are non-negotiable — miss them and you're replaced
- 信创 compliance is mandatory, not optional — budget must be allocated regardless of ROI
- 三重一大 means every large purchase requires collective decision — CFO cannot unilaterally approve
- 海康威视 CFO balances commercial optimization with state-owner reporting requirements

**出海合规型 (Cross-Border Compliance):**
- Multi-jurisdiction tax structuring is full-time job (Shein: China manufacturing, Singapore HQ, global sales)
- VIE structures create ongoing compliance risk — any regulatory shift is existential
- PCAOB audit access negotiations directly impact listing viability
- WeBull manages regulatory capital across multiple financial jurisdictions simultaneously

| KPI | Metric | China-Specific Note |

## §4 Pain Points

### Pain Point 1: 回款难 (Collection Difficulty)
**Description:** China's business culture normalizes payment delays. A signed contract with agreed 30-day terms often means 90-120 days in practice. 拖欠 (delayed payment) is endemic, especially from SOE and large enterprise customers.
**Impact:** Cash flow planning becomes probabilistic rather than deterministic. CFO must maintain credit lines to bridge gaps. Startup CFOs at companies like 禾观 face existential risk from single-customer payment delays.
**Emotional weight:** Frustration, helplessness — legal remedies are slow and relationship-damaging.

### Pain Point 2: 多头监管 (Multi-Regulator Burden)
**Description:** A dual-listed Chinese company may face simultaneous oversight from: 证监会 (CSRC), SEC, HKEX, 税务局 (SAT), 外管局 (SAFE), 审计署, local government, SASAC (if SOE). Each has different reporting requirements, timelines, and formats.
**Impact:** Enormous compliance team cost. Contradictory requirements (e.g., PCAOB demands audit access that conflicts with Chinese data security laws). WeBull faces this acutely — financial services license requirements across US, HK, Singapore, Australia.
**Emotional weight:** Overwhelm, fear of missing a requirement and facing personal liability.

### Pain Point 3: 创始人越权 (Founder Override)
**Description:** In 民企, founders often bypass CFO-established processes — approving expenditures directly, making financial commitments without CFO knowledge, maintaining relationships with banks personally.
**Impact:** CFO cannot maintain internal controls, faces audit risk, personal liability for financial statements they don't fully control. At ByteDance scale, the finance function must serve founder vision while maintaining $110B+ revenue governance.
**Emotional weight:** Emasculation, career risk, caught between founder loyalty and professional duty.

### Pain Point 4: 信创替代的投资回报不确定性 (Uncertain ROI of Xinchuang Substitution)
**Description:** Mandated replacement of foreign tech (Oracle → 用友, SAP → 金蝶, Salesforce → domestic CRM) often results in capability regression. CFO must budget millions for compliance-driven migration with negative near-term ROI.
**Impact:** Finance system degradation during transition. Consolidation accuracy drops. Reporting timelines extend. Budget consumed by compliance rather than value-creation.
**Emotional weight:** Resentment at unfunded mandate; anxiety about operational disruption.

### Pain Point 5: 人才短缺 (Finance Talent Shortage)
**Description:** Qualified finance professionals who understand both IFRS/GAAP and Chinese accounting standards, speak English, and have IPO experience are extremely scarce. Competition for talent is fierce among pre-IPO companies.
**Impact:** CFO personally carries knowledge that should be distributed across team. Key-person risk. Burnout. At Shein's scale ($30B+ revenue), building a finance team that can handle IPO scrutiny while maintaining startup speed is nearly impossible.
**Emotional weight:** Loneliness, overwork, inability to delegate critical functions.

### Pain Point 6: 政策不确定性 (Policy Uncertainty)
**Description:** Regulatory changes can be sudden and transformative — VIE structure legality shifts, 数据安全法 implementation, PCAOB access negotiations, 双减 (education sector crackdown), gaming time limits (腾讯Game). CFO must maintain contingency plans for discontinuous regulatory risk.
**Impact:** Financial planning requires scenario modeling for policy shifts. Capital structure may need emergency restructuring. Investor confidence depends on CFO demonstrating policy navigation capability.
**Emotional weight:** Anxiety, hypervigilance, difficulty making long-term commitments.

### Pain Point 7: 内部损益分配争议 (Internal P&L Allocation Disputes)
**Description:** In decentralized structures like 海尔's 人单合一, CFO must design and maintain internal transfer pricing, cost allocation, and performance measurement systems that satisfy multiple business unit leaders. At 腾讯Game (division within HK:0700), massive internal budget allocation decisions lack external market validation.
**Impact:** Political conflicts consume CFO bandwidth. Business unit leaders game the system. Allocation methodology always feels arbitrary to someone. CFO becomes referee in zero-sum internal competition.
**Emotional weight:** Political exhaustion, inability to satisfy all stakeholders.

### Pain Point 8: 审计关系管理 (Audit Relationship Management)
**Description:** Choosing between Big 4 (credibility for listing, expensive, potentially PCAOB-compromised) and domestic firms (cheaper, regulatory preference for SOE, less international credibility). Post-IPO, switching auditors signals trouble. PCAOB inspection standoff creates ongoing uncertainty for US-listed Chinese companies.
**Impact:** Audit fees are substantial line item. Auditor demands drive internal control investments. 猎豹 (NYSE-listed) must maintain US audit standards while managing declining revenue — auditors scrutinize going-concern assumptions.
**Emotional weight:** Dependence on auditor goodwill; fear of qualified opinion.

## §5 Objection Patterns

### Objection 1: "我们现在没有预算" (We don't have budget right now)

**What they mean:** This is either (a) literally true — budget exhausted for fiscal year, (b) polite rejection, or (c) testing whether you can solve their budget-cycle problem.

**Underlying dynamic:** China budget cycles are rigid. SOEs lock budgets in Q1 aligned with SASAC targets. 民企 may have rolling budgets but founder approval is required for unplanned spend. Startups genuinely may have no discretionary budget.

**Response strategy:** Identify which sub-type. If (a): align to next budget cycle, offer Q4 pilot at current-year pricing. If (b): probe for real objection — "如果预算不是问题, 这个方案对您有吸引力吗?" If (c): offer creative commercial structures — multi-year, deferred payment, success-based pricing.

### Objection 2: "需要走流程/需要集体决策" (Need to follow process / collective decision)

**What they mean:** In SOE context, this is 100% real — 三重一大 requires committee approval for significant expenditure. In 民企 context, it often means "I need founder approval and I'm not sure I can get it."

**Underlying dynamic:** CFO may genuinely support your solution but lacks unilateral authority. The 流程 may take 3-6 months in SOE. For 海康威视, procurement above certain thresholds requires CETC parent approval.

**Response strategy:** Offer to support their internal justification process. Provide ROI materials formatted for their decision-making framework. Ask: "我能帮您准备什么材料来支持内部审批?" Patience is non-negotiable.

### Objection 3: "ROI不够清晰" (ROI isn't clear enough)

**What they mean:** China CFOs are trained to demand quantified returns. Soft benefits (efficiency, employee satisfaction) carry almost zero weight. They need hard numbers they can put in a 立项报告 (project approval report).

**Underlying dynamic:** CFO's internal credibility depends on every approved project delivering measurable return. A failed investment haunts them at next 考核. Risk aversion is rational.

**Response strategy:** Co-create ROI model using THEIR data. Offer pilot/POC with defined success metrics before full commitment. Reference comparable Chinese company results (not Western case studies — irrelevant). "我们可以先做一个小规模验证, 用您的实际数据来建模。"

### Objection 4: "我们在看国产替代方案" (We're evaluating domestic alternatives)

**What they mean:** Either (a) 信创 mandate requires domestic solutions, (b) using domestic alternative as price leverage, or (c) genuinely believe domestic solutions are catching up.

**Underlying dynamic:** 信创 is real policy pressure for SOE and government-adjacent companies. However, many 民企 use "信创" as negotiation tactic to extract discounts from foreign vendors. The CFO knows domestic solutions are often inferior but may be politically required.

**Response strategy:** For SOE/政策型: accept reality, offer hybrid approach or partnership with domestic vendor. For 民企: probe whether mandate is real or negotiating tactic — "这是合规要求还是成本考量?" If cost: compete on TCO. If compliance: adapt.

### Objection 5: "现在时机不对, 我们在准备上市" (Timing is wrong, we're preparing for IPO)

**What they mean:** IPO preparation consumes 100% of CFO bandwidth for 12-18 months. Any new system implementation creates audit risk (new processes = new control gaps to document). They're in lockdown mode.

**Underlying dynamic:** Pre-IPO CFO (like Shein's finance leadership currently) is terrified of anything that disrupts clean financials or adds complexity auditors will question. Conversely, solutions that SUPPORT IPO readiness get fast approval.

**Response strategy:** Reframe your solution as IPO-enabling rather than IPO-distracting. "我们的方案实际上能帮助您加速IPO准备 — 自动化内控报告, 满足审计要求。" If genuinely irrelevant to IPO: park the conversation, establish relationship, return post-listing.

### Objection 6: "我不是最终决策人" (I'm not the final decision-maker)

**What they mean:** In 创始人钱袋子型 companies, this is painfully honest — the CFO literally cannot approve significant spend without founder sign-off. They're warning you that selling to them alone is insufficient.

**Underlying dynamic:** CFO may be ally but not buyer. They can block (by not recommending) but cannot approve (without founder). At Anker, major financial system decisions ultimately need 阳萌's nod.

**Response strategy:** Acknowledge and enlist. "我理解, 那我怎么能帮助您向[决策人]展示这个方案的价值?" Make the CFO your internal champion by reducing their effort to advocate. Provide founder-ready materials (concise, vision-aligned, not technical).

### Objection 7: "数据安全怎么保证?" (How do you guarantee data security?)

**What they mean:** Post-数据安全法 and 个人信息保护法, CFOs face personal criminal liability for data breaches. Cross-border data transfer is heavily regulated (数据出境安全评估). This is not abstract compliance — it's career-survival.

**Underlying dynamic:** Heightened since DiDi's $1.2B fine. CFOs at companies like 海康威视 (on US entity list) or Shein (complex data flows between China/Singapore/global) are acutely sensitive. WeBull manages financial data across jurisdictions with strict requirements.

**Response strategy:** Lead with compliance architecture, not features. Show data residency, 等保三级 certification, data localization capabilities. "我们的方案完全符合数据安全法和等保要求, 数据不出境。" Have legal/compliance documentation ready.

### Objection 8: "我们内部可以开发" (We can build this internally)

**What they mean:** China's engineering talent pool makes "build vs buy" lean heavily toward "build" — especially at tech companies. ByteDance, 腾讯, 海尔 all have massive internal development capabilities. CFO may genuinely believe internal development is cheaper.

**Underlying dynamic:** Internal development is favored because (a) it avoids procurement process, (b) no recurring SaaS fees on P&L, (c) 信创-compliant by default, (d) gives CFO control. However, they underestimate maintenance cost, opportunity cost of engineering resources, and time-to-value.

**Response strategy:** Don't argue against building — argue for speed and total cost. "自建需要多长时间? 这段时间的机会成本是多少? 我们3个月内上线, 您的团队可以专注核心业务。" Quantify fully-loaded internal development cost honestly.

## §6 Buying Dynamics

### How CFOs Buy in China

#### Budget Authority Spectrum

| Archetype | Typical Authority | Approval Threshold | Above-Threshold Process |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| 创始人钱袋子型 | ¥50K-500K autonomous | ¥500K+ needs founder | WeChat message to founder → verbal approval → proceed |
| 资本市场型 | ¥1M-5M autonomous | ¥5M+ needs board/committee | Formal 立项 → budget review → board consent (1-3 months) |
| 政策型/体制型 | ¥100K-1M autonomous | ¥1M+ = 三重一大 | Committee meeting → Party committee → parent company → approve (3-6 months) |
| 出海合规型 | ¥500K-2M autonomous | ¥2M+ needs regional/global | Business case → regional approval → global alignment (2-4 months) |
| 烧钱续命型 | ¥10K-100K autonomous | ¥100K+ needs CEO/board | Board meeting → investor consultation → approve (1-2 months) |
| 分权损益型 | Varies by BU | Cross-BU = group level | BU CFO → Group CFO → steering committee (2-3 months) |

#### Procurement Process (Enterprise China)

1. **Need identification** — Usually from business unit, sometimes CFO-initiated for finance-stack
2. **立项申请** (Project initiation) — Formal document with budget estimate, ROI projection, alternatives analysis
3. **供应商筛选** (Vendor shortlisting) — Often requires 3+ quotes (SOE: mandatory competitive bidding above threshold)
4. **Technical evaluation** — IT/business team conducts; CFO not involved in details
5. **商务谈判** (Commercial negotiation) — CFO or procurement leads; price is king in China
6. **Internal approval** — Per authority matrix above
7. **合同签署** (Contract signing) — Legal review, stamp (公章) application
8. **实施** (Implementation) — CFO monitors budget adherence
9. **验收** (Acceptance) — Formal sign-off triggers payment (often 70/30 or 80/20 split)

#### How CFO Blocks Deals

- **Silent veto:** Simply doesn't advance 立项 document — deal dies in pipeline without explicit rejection
- **Budget redirect:** "We've allocated that budget to [higher priority]" — cannot be argued against
- **ROI challenge:** Demands impossible-to-prove returns, knowing you can't deliver them
- **Timing delay:** "Let's revisit next fiscal year" — kills deal momentum without saying no
- **Procurement expansion:** Adds requirements that only a competitor (or no one) can meet
- **Risk escalation:** Flags data security, compliance, or vendor stability concerns to CTO/CEO

#### How CFO Enables Deals

- **Budget creation:** Identifies unused budget lines or creates new allocation for compelling solutions
- **Fast-track authority:** Uses autonomous approval for deals below threshold — can happen in days
- **Internal sponsorship:** Champions the vendor in cross-functional meetings
- **Commercial creativity:** Structures deal to fit available budget (multi-year, milestone-based, pilot-to-full)
- **Regulatory framing:** Positions purchase as compliance-required (信创, 数据安全, 内控) to bypass ROI scrutiny

#### Price Sensitivity & Negotiation Culture

China CFOs negotiate aggressively. Expect:
- **First offer rejected always** — Build in 15-20% negotiation margin
- **Competitive quoting** — Even if they prefer you, they'll obtain competitor quotes for leverage
- **Volume/term commitments** in exchange for discount — multi-year deals are normal
- **Payment terms as negotiation lever** — They'll ask for 90-day terms or milestone-based payment
- **Pilot as price-discovery** — Small initial deal to establish reference price for expansion

### CFO's Relationship with Procurement (采购)

In SOE and large enterprises, 采购部 (procurement department) is a separate function with independent authority. CFO sets budget ceiling and financial policy; procurement executes within those constraints. In 民企, procurement often reports to CFO or is informal. Key insight: **In SOE, you must satisfy both CFO (budget owner) and procurement (process owner).** They have different incentives — CFO wants value, procurement wants compliance and price.

## §7 Discovery Questions

### Opening Questions (Build Rapport & Identify Archetype)

1. "您目前的财务团队规模和组织架构是怎样的?" 
   *(Purpose: Assesses maturity; <10 people = startup/founder-controlled; 100+ = mature enterprise)*

2. "贵公司目前最大的财务管理挑战是什么?"
   *(Purpose: Open-ended, lets them reveal archetype through answer pattern)*

3. "您的预算审批流程是怎样的? 一般需要多长时间?"
   *(Purpose: Directly surfaces authority level and process complexity)*

### Deepening Questions (Surface Pain Points)

4. "回款周期管理方面, 您目前面临什么困难?"
   *(Purpose: 回款 is universal pain; their response reveals cash flow pressure intensity)*

5. "在合规和审计方面, 您今年最关注哪些变化?"
   *(Purpose: Surfaces regulatory pressure — 信创, 数据安全, PCAOB, ESG)*

6. "贵公司的税务优化策略是怎么考虑的? 高新企业认定、研发加计扣除这些都在用吗?"
   *(Purpose: Assesses sophistication; also signals you understand their world)*

7. "您如何平衡业务增长需求和成本控制之间的关系?"
   *(Purpose: Reveals their position in CEO↔Investor pull dynamic)*

### Qualifying Questions (Assess Deal Viability)

8. "这类项目在您这里的决策流程是怎样的? 还需要哪些人参与?"
   *(Purpose: Maps decision-making unit; identifies if CFO is buyer or influencer)*

9. "您目前有相关预算吗? 还是需要单独立项?"
   *(Purpose: Directly assesses budget availability and procurement timeline)*

10. "上次您批准类似规模的项目, 从评估到落地大概用了多长时间?"
    *(Purpose: Sets realistic timeline expectations based on their historical pattern)*

### Advanced Questions (For Specific Archetypes)

**For 资本市场型:**
11. "投资者对贵公司的财务透明度有什么具体反馈?"
12. "您在管理市场预期方面最大的挑战是什么?"

**For 政策型/体制型:**
13. "国资委的考核指标今年有什么变化?"
14. "信创替代对您的财务系统有什么影响?"

**For 出海合规型:**
15. "跨境资金管理方面, 您目前用什么方案?"
16. "不同司法管辖区的合规要求如何协调?"

**For 创始人钱袋子型:**
17. "创始人对财务管理的数字化有什么期望?"
18. "您如何向创始人展示财务投资的价值?"

## §8 Communication Style

### General China CFO Communication Preferences

| Dimension | Preference | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Language** | Chinese (Mandarin) for substance; English acceptable for global/HK-listed | Nuance matters in finance; they think in Chinese even if bilingual |
| **Channel** | WeChat (initial contact) → 企业微信/Email (formal) → Face-to-face (decision) | WeChat is universal first-contact; formal channels for audit trail |
| **Format** | Data-first, then narrative. Tables > paragraphs. | CFOs are numbers people — lead with quantified impact |
| **Length** | Concise — one page executive summary + detailed appendix | They're time-poor; respect this by frontloading key message |
| **Frequency** | Monthly touchpoints; weekly during active evaluation | Don't over-contact; Chinese business values 分寸 (appropriate measure) |
| **Meeting style** | 30-min focused meetings; avoid "relationship lunches" until deal is progressing | CFOs less relationship-driven than CEOs; they value efficiency |

### Communication Style by Archetype

**创始人钱袋子型:**
- Communicate through founder relationship when possible
- CFO responds to founder's endorsement faster than independent outreach
- Practical, execution-focused — "how does this work" not "why should we"
- WeChat voice messages acceptable for quick decisions

**资本市场型:**
- Professional, structured communication — they're trained by investment banks
- Quarterly rhythm — align outreach to earnings cycle (avoid blackout periods)
- Benchmarking language works: "同行业XX公司已经在用..."
- Fluent in English financial terminology; code-switching is natural

**政策型/体制型:**
- Formal, documented communication — everything may be audited
- Avoid pressure tactics — they cannot accelerate 流程 even if they want to
- Respect hierarchy — initial contact may need to go through 办公室 (general office)
- Title and seniority consciousness — match meeting seniority levels

**出海合规型:**
- Multi-lingual, internationally experienced — direct communication works
- Appreciate understanding of their cross-border complexity
- Legal/compliance precision in language — vague claims trigger suspicion
- Often jet-lagged/timezone-challenged — be flexible on meeting times

**烧钱续命型:**
- Speed matters — they're burning cash daily and feel urgency
- Commercial flexibility signals: payment terms, pilot pricing, success-based fees
- Direct and informal — startup culture carries into finance function
- May respond at odd hours — founder culture means always-on

**分权损益型:**
- Navigate carefully — may need to engage both Group CFO and BU finance leads
- Internal political awareness is essential — don't accidentally champion one BU over another
- Standardization messaging works: "帮助您统一全集团的财务视角"
- Consensus-building takes time; provide materials that BU leads can all support

### What Resonates vs. What Repels

| ✅ Resonates | ❌ Repels |
|-------------|----------|
| Quantified ROI with China benchmarks | Vague "transformation" language without numbers |
| Understanding of their regulatory context | Generic global case studies without China relevance |
| Flexible commercial terms | Rigid pricing with no negotiation space |
| Risk mitigation framing | Pure growth/innovation messaging (that's for CEO) |
| Compliance-enabling language | Anything that implies current non-compliance |
| Peer references from similar Chinese companies | Western-company references (irrelevant context) |
| Patience with their decision process | Artificial urgency or quarter-end pressure |
| Technical depth when requested | Surface-level features without financial rigor |
| Data security certifications upfront | Downplaying data residency concerns |
| Chinese-language materials, localized UX | English-only documentation for Chinese deployment |

### Emotional Register

China CFOs project **rationality and control** — emotions are rarely displayed in professional settings. However, beneath the surface:

- **Fear** drives 60% of decisions (fear of audit failure, fear of founder displeasure, fear of missing SASAC targets, fear of personal liability)
- **Pride** in professional expertise — they want to be seen as sophisticated, not just bookkeepers
- **Isolation** — CFO role is inherently lonely (guardian of uncomfortable truths)
- **Pragmatism** — less ideological than Western CFOs about "digital transformation"; they want things that work

**Engagement implication:** Sell to the fear (risk reduction, compliance assurance, audit readiness) rather than aspiration. But respect the pride — never imply their current approach is inadequate. Position as "enhancement" not "fix."
