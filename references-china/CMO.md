## 1. Why This Persona Matters

The China-market CMO (or more commonly: 增长VP, 品牌VP, 市场VP) controls brand perception, customer acquisition cost, and increasingly — revenue attribution. In Chinese tech and consumer companies, this role sits at the intersection of:

- **Brand narrative** — especially critical during 出海 (going overseas) or premium repositioning
- **Performance marketing** — measured in real-time ROI via platforms like 巨量引擎 (Ocean Engine), 腾讯广告, and 磁力引擎
- **私域流量 (private domain traffic)** — the WeChat/企业微信 ecosystem that Western marketers have no equivalent for
- **直播带货 (livestream commerce)** — a conversion channel that merges content, commerce, and community

**Key structural difference from Western CMOs:** In China, the CMO role has fragmented. Many companies split it into a Growth VP (performance/数据驱动) and a Brand VP (品牌/公关). Some founder-CEOs act as their own chief marketer. The "unified CMO" archetype is rarer, and tenure averages 18–24 months — the shortest of any C-suite role.

**Why vendors must understand this persona:** Any MarTech, AdTech, CDP, or creative-tech vendor selling into China must speak the language of 品效合一 (brand-performance integration), understand platform-specific constraints (个保法/PIPL limiting targeting), and demonstrate measurable lift within one budget cycle.

## 2. Title Variants and Reporting Lines

| Common Title | Chinese | Context |
|---|---|---|
| CMO | 首席营销官 | Rare in pure-play tech; more common in MNCs and consumer brands |
| VP of Brand | 品牌VP / 品牌负责人 | Owns narrative, PR, positioning |
| VP of Growth | 增长VP / 用户增长负责人 | Owns acquisition, retention, monetization loops |
| VP of Marketing | 市场VP / 市场总监 | Traditional title, mid-size companies |
| Head of 私域 | 私域负责人 | Dedicated WeChat/community leader (reports to CMO or COO) |
| CGO (Chief Growth Officer) | 首席增长官 | Emerged ~2019, combines marketing + product growth |

**Reporting line:** Typically reports to CEO or COO. In founder-led companies (理想汽车, 猎豹移动), the founder often absorbs CMO functions directly. In matrix orgs (海尔), marketing is decentralized to business units under 人单合一 model.

**Budget authority:** Controls 5–15% of revenue in consumer companies, 2–8% in B2B/tech. Performance budget is often 60–70% of total, with brand/content taking the remainder.

## 3. Archetypes

### Archetype A: The Data-Driven Growth Engine (数据驱动增长型)

**Profile:** Former product manager or data scientist who moved into marketing. Lives in dashboards. Thinks in funnels, cohorts, and LTV/CAC ratios. Runs rapid experiments across channels.

**Illustrative pattern — Anker model:**
Anker built its multi-brand empire (Anker/Soundcore/eufy) on Amazon-native data loops. Every product launch is preceded by keyword analysis, review mining, and competitive gap identification. Marketing spend is allocated algorithmically. The CMO-equivalent role here is less "creative visionary" and more "growth systems architect."

- Manages 50+ SKUs across brands with distinct positioning
- Amazon PPC optimization is a core competency, not outsourced
- DTC channels (ankertechnology.com) tested via A/B frameworks
- Success metric: contribution margin per marketing dollar, not impressions

**What they buy:** Attribution platforms, CDP/DMP solutions, creative automation tools, A/B testing infrastructure, cross-channel analytics.

**Language they use:**
- "ROI per channel per SKU per week"
- "我们不做品牌广告,只做效果" (We don't do brand ads, only performance)
- "LTV:CAC ratio must be > 3:1 by month 6"
- "Show me incrementality, not correlation"

**How to sell to them:** Lead with data. Show controlled experiments. Provide API-first integration. Never lead with "brand awareness" — translate everything into revenue impact.

---

### Archetype B: The Platform Ecosystem Builder (平台生态营销型)

**Profile:** Understands that in China, you don't just *use* platforms — you build ecosystems on them. This archetype builds self-reinforcing marketing flywheels where content, commerce, and community feed each other.

**Illustrative pattern — ByteDance/巨量引擎 model:**
ByteDance doesn't just sell ads — it built an entire marketing operating system (巨量引擎/Ocean Engine) where advertisers create content, target audiences, convert sales, and retarget — all without leaving the ecosystem. The marketing leader in this model thinks in terms of "ecosystem lock-in" and "platform-native content."

- 巨量千川 for e-commerce traffic, 巨量星图 for KOL matching
- Content IS the ad — native video outperforms interruption-based formats 3–5x
- Closed-loop attribution within platform (impression → click → purchase)
- 抖音电商 GMV growth drives marketing strategy, not the reverse

**What they buy:** Platform-native tools, creator management platforms, livestream infrastructure, social listening tools, content intelligence systems.

**Language they use:**
- "内容即广告,广告即内容" (Content is ads, ads are content)
- "We need 全域营销 (omni-domain marketing), not just 公域 (public domain)"
- "Our 自播 (self-broadcast) room needs 3x GMV per session"
- "星图达人 ROI is declining — we need more素人 (grassroots) content"

**How to sell to them:** Demonstrate platform integration depth. Show how your tool amplifies platform-native behaviors. Speak their platform vocabulary. Offer benchmarks against category peers on the same platform.

### Archetype C: The Founder-as-CMO (创始人即品牌型)

**Profile:** The CEO who IS the brand. Personal IP drives company awareness. Every Weibo post, WeChat article, or 抖音 video is a marketing asset. The "CMO" role below them is execution-focused.

**Illustrative pattern — 理想汽车/Li Auto (李想) model:**
李想 (Li Xiang) personally writes product announcements, responds to customer complaints on WeChat, and sets the brand tone. His personal reputation — analytical, direct, product-obsessed — IS the 理想 brand. Marketing team executes his vision but doesn't set strategy.

Similarly, 猎豹移动's Fu Sheng built his personal brand as a thought leader — speaking at conferences, writing long-form content, positioning himself as an AI visionary — which became inseparable from company positioning.

- CEO's personal social following > company official accounts
- Every crisis is personal; every win is personal
- Marketing VP role is "chief executor" not "chief strategist"
- Brand consistency = CEO consistency

**What they buy (or rather, what their teams buy):** Social media management tools, reputation monitoring, ghostwriting/content services, video production, crisis monitoring systems.

**Language the founder uses:**
- "我亲自来写" (I'll write it myself)
- "品牌就是创始人的延伸" (Brand is an extension of the founder)
- "Don't give me a campaign deck — give me talking points"
- "用户在哪里,我就在哪里" (Where users are, I am)

**How to sell to them:** You're selling to the execution team, not the founder. Make their job easier. Provide tools that help them scale the founder's voice without diluting it. Offer monitoring/analytics that keep the founder informed without requiring their time.

---

### Archetype D: The Private-Domain Architect (私域流量操盘手)

**Profile:** Obsessed with building owned audiences that bypass platform algorithms. WeChat (个人号 + 企业微信 + 小程序 + 视频号) is their primary canvas. Thinks in "user lifecycle within private domain."

**Illustrative pattern — 海尔/Casarte premium positioning:**
海尔's 人单合一 model pushes marketing responsibility to individual "小微" (micro-enterprises). Each sub-brand (Casarte for premium, Leader for youth) maintains its own private domain communities. Casarte builds WeChat groups for high-net-worth homeowners, provides concierge-style content, and converts through 小程序 mini-programs.

- 企业微信 manages 1:1 relationships at scale
- WeChat Moments ads drive into private domain, not websites
- Community managers (社群运营) are the "last mile" of marketing
- Retention/repurchase > acquisition in budget allocation

**What they buy:** SCRM (Social CRM) platforms, 企业微信 extensions, mini-program development, community management tools, WeChat analytics, loyalty/membership platforms.

**Language they use:**
- "公域获客,私域养鱼" (Acquire in public domain, nurture in private domain)
- "每个用户的LTV要算清楚" (Calculate every user's LTV clearly)
- "企微好友数是我们的核心资产" (Enterprise WeChat contacts are our core asset)
- "我们要的不是流量,是留量" (We don't want traffic/流量, we want retention/留量 — a pun)
- "私域复购率要做到40%以上" (Private domain repurchase rate must exceed 40%)

**How to sell to them:** Show WeChat ecosystem integration. Demonstrate user journey from 公域 to 私域 with measurable conversion. Provide case studies with repurchase rate improvements. Never propose solutions that bypass WeChat — work within it.

---

### Archetype E: The Globalization Storyteller (出海品牌叙事型)

**Profile:** Tasked with building a Chinese brand's reputation in overseas markets. Must bridge Chinese product DNA with local market sensibilities. Manages dual identity — Chinese innovation story for domestic pride, localized narrative for foreign consumers.

**Illustrative pattern — Shein social-first model:**
Shein built a $60B+ brand with virtually zero traditional advertising. Instead: micro-influencer army (KOL/KOC) on Instagram/TikTok, gamified app experience, ultra-fast trend response (small batch → test → scale), and affiliate-driven acquisition. The marketing leader here is part data scientist, part cultural anthropologist.

**Illustrative pattern — 腾讯 Games/Level Infinite:**
腾讯 Games created "Level Infinite" as a publishing brand for overseas markets — deliberately de-coupling from the 腾讯 parent brand to avoid geopolitical friction while leveraging IP marketing expertise (Honor of Kings → international versions). Esports investment builds brand equity without traditional advertising.

- TikTok/Meta/Google as primary overseas channels (出海三件套)
- Influencer marketing scaled via platforms, not agencies
- Brand building through community, not broadcast
- Cultural adaptation > translation

**What they buy:** Cross-border marketing platforms, influencer management tools, localization services, global analytics/attribution, brand tracking studies, social listening (multilingual).

**Language they use:**
- "出海不是翻译,是重新讲故事" (Going overseas isn't translation, it's retelling the story)
- "We need local-first creative, not adapted Chinese creative"
- "TikTok Shop is our next growth vector"
- "品牌出海要先出圈,再出海" (Brand globalization: break out of your circle first, then go overseas)
- "ROI by market by channel by creator tier"

**How to sell to them:** Show cross-market expertise. Demonstrate understanding of both Chinese brand DNA and local market dynamics. Provide multi-market dashboards. Offer creator/influencer networks in target markets. Show regulatory compliance (GDPR, local ad laws).

---

### Archetype F: The Brand-Recovery Specialist (品牌修复/重塑型)

**Profile:** Brought in after crisis, scandal, or prolonged brand decline. Mandate is reputation rebuild + commercial recovery. High-pressure, short-timeline, measurable outcomes required.

**Illustrative pattern — Cathay Pacific brand recovery:**
After multiple controversies affecting mainland China perception, Cathay Pacific undertook systematic brand recovery: revitalizing Asia Miles loyalty program, premium positioning reinforcement, and careful stakeholder communications. The CMO-equivalent must balance multiple audiences (mainland Chinese travelers, Hong Kong locals, international flyers) with conflicting expectations.

- Crisis communications playbook is always ready
- Sentiment monitoring is real-time, not monthly
- Loyalty/membership programs as brand trust rebuilders
- Partnership/co-branding to borrow credibility
- Progress measured in NPS recovery, booking recovery, social sentiment scores

**What they buy:** Reputation management platforms, real-time social listening, PR agency retainers, customer sentiment analytics, loyalty platform technology, crisis simulation tools.

**Language they use:**
- "品牌信任重建需要每一个触点都对" (Brand trust rebuilding requires every touchpoint to be right)
- "我们不能只看声量,要看情感倾向" (We can't just look at volume, must look at sentiment)
- "Give me weekly NPS movement, not quarterly"
- "每一次客户互动都是品牌修复的机会" (Every customer interaction is a brand recovery opportunity)

**How to sell to them:** Lead with speed-to-insight. Show real-time monitoring capabilities. Demonstrate crisis response workflows. Provide competitive sentiment benchmarks. Show how quickly you can detect and alert on negative narrative shifts.

## 4. KPIs and Metrics That Matter

### Universal Metrics (All Archetypes)

| Metric | Chinese Term | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Customer Acquisition Cost | 获客成本 (CAC) | The single most scrutinized number in board reviews |
| Lifetime Value | 用户生命周期价值 (LTV) | Justifies acquisition spend; LTV:CAC > 3 is baseline |
| Brand-Performance Ratio | 品效比 | How much brand spend converts to measurable outcomes |
| GMV Attribution | GMV归因 | What marketing drove in actual sales |
| Private Domain User Count | 私域用户数 | Size of owned audience (企业微信 contacts, mini-program users) |
| Repurchase Rate | 复购率 | Especially in private domain; target 30–50% |
| Content ROI | 内容投产比 | Revenue per content piece or campaign |

### Platform-Specific Metrics

| Platform | Key Metric | Benchmark Range |
|---|---|---|
| 抖音/巨量千川 | GPM (GMV per mille impressions) | ¥800–3,000 depending on category |
| 小红书 | 种草转化率 (seeding conversion) | 2–8% click-through to purchase |
| 企业微信私域 | 好友通过率 + 留存率 | 60%+ acceptance, 80%+ 7-day retention |
| 直播 (livestream) | 场均GMV / 投产比 | ROI 1:3 to 1:8 target |
| 微信视频号 | 公域撬动比 | Private domain seed → public amplification ratio |

### Board-Level Metrics

- **Marketing efficiency ratio** (营销费用率): Marketing spend as % of revenue. Benchmark: 8–15% consumer, 3–8% tech
- **Brand awareness lift**: Measured quarterly via survey panels (秒针/iResearch)
- **Share of voice** (SOV): Category share of paid + earned media
- **NPS / 净推荐值**: Customer advocacy indicator
- **DAU/MAU of owned channels**: Mini-program, app, video号 follower growth

## 5. Decision-Making Framework

### How China-Market CMOs Evaluate Vendors

**Stage 1: Problem recognition (问题识别)**
- Internal trigger: CAC rising, ROI declining, new platform emerging, boss saw competitor doing something
- External trigger: Platform policy change (e.g., 抖音 algorithm shift), regulation (个保法 enforcement), market disruption

**Stage 2: Solution exploration (方案探索)**
- Ask peers in CMO WeChat groups (圈子很小 — the circle is small)
- Check vendor's 小红书/抖音 case studies
- Look for category-specific proof points (same industry, same scale)
- Evaluate via POC/pilot (试用期) — usually 1–3 months

**Stage 3: Internal selling (内部推动)**
- Must convince CEO/CFO with ROI projection
- Procurement (采购) involvement for deals > ¥500K annually
- IT/tech team must validate integration feasibility
- Legal reviews data compliance (个保法/数据安全法)

**Stage 4: Decision criteria (决策标准)**

| Criterion | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Proven ROI in similar company | 30% | "谁在用?效果怎样?" (Who uses it? What results?) |
| Platform integration depth | 25% | Must connect to existing stack (企微/抖音/小红书) |
| Speed to value | 20% | Must show results within 1 quarter |
| Price/value ratio | 15% | Not cheapest — but must justify vs alternatives |
| Vendor stability/support | 10% | Local team, Chinese-language support, responsive |

**Key insight:** Chinese CMOs decide FAST (2–6 weeks for mid-size deals) but churn vendors frequently. Proving value in the first 30 days is critical to retention.

## 6. Pain Points and Frustrations

### Structural Pain Points

1. **平台依赖症 (Platform dependency):** Over-reliance on 抖音/小红书 means algorithm changes can tank performance overnight. CMOs crave channel diversification but budgets follow proven ROI.

2. **品效难合一 (Brand-performance integration is hard):** Everyone talks about 品效合一 but few achieve it. Brand campaigns are hard to attribute; performance campaigns erode brand equity. The tension is real and unresolved.

3. **数据孤岛 (Data silos):** Each platform guards its data. Building a unified customer view across 抖音+微信+淘宝+线下 is technically and legally challenging post-个保法.

4. **人才短缺 (Talent shortage):** Good 投手 (media buyers), 内容创作者 (content creators), and 私域运营 (private domain operators) are expensive and mobile. Team instability is constant.

5. **短期主义 (Short-termism):** 18–24 month average tenure means CMOs optimize for quick wins. Long-term brand building is sacrificed for quarterly performance.

6. **合规压力 (Compliance pressure):** 个保法 (PIPL), 广告法, 反不正当竞争法 — regulations constrain targeting, claims, and data usage. Fines are increasing.

### Emotional Frustrations

- "Every quarter the CEO asks why our 抖音 ROI dropped, but won't invest in brand"
- "We built 500K 企微好友 but can't prove they drive revenue vs. organic"
- "Our competitor went viral on 小红书 and now boss wants us to copy them"
- "Agency提案 (proposals) all look the same — no one understands our specific business"
- "IT says data integration takes 6 months; I need results in 6 weeks"

## 7. Regulatory and Policy Context

### Key Regulations Affecting Marketing

| Regulation | Chinese Name | Impact on CMO |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Information Protection Law | 个人信息保护法 (个保法/PIPL) | Limits ad targeting, requires consent, restricts cross-platform data sharing |
| Advertising Law | 广告法 | Restricts superlatives ("最好/第一"), health claims, comparative advertising |
| Data Security Law | 数据安全法 | Cross-border data transfer restrictions affect global campaigns |
| Anti-Unfair Competition Law | 反不正当竞争法 | Restricts fake reviews, paid rankings, misleading promotions |
| E-Commerce Law | 电子商务法 | Livestream hosts must disclose commercial relationships |
| Cybersecurity Law | 网络安全法 | Data localization requirements |

### 15th Five-Year Plan Early Signals (2026–2030)

- **Digital consumption upgrading** (数字消费升级): Policy support for digital marketing innovation
- **Data element marketization** (数据要素市场化): May create new compliant data-sharing frameworks
- **Cultural confidence** (文化自信): Domestic brands encouraged; "国潮" (China-chic) trend has policy tailwinds
- **Green consumption** (绿色消费): ESG messaging becoming important for premium brands

## 9. Communication Preferences

### How to Reach Them

| Channel | Effectiveness | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| WeChat (微信) | ★★★★★ | THE business channel. Warm intro required. |
| 行业活动/峰会 | ★★★★☆ | Marketing summits, platform events (巨量大会, 腾讯智慧营销) |
| 小红书/抖音 content | ★★★★☆ | They consume platform content; thought leadership works |
| Cold email | ★☆☆☆☆ | Almost never read; not part of workflow |
| Phone call (cold) | ★☆☆☆☆ | Considered rude without prior WeChat connection |
| LinkedIn/脉脉 | ★★★☆☆ | Emerging for B2B; 脉脉 for domestic networking |

### Content That Resonates

- **Case studies with numbers:** "某美妆品牌3个月私域GMV增长300%" (A beauty brand grew private domain GMV 300% in 3 months)
- **Platform trend reports:** "2026抖音电商趋势白皮书" (2026 Douyin E-commerce Trend White Paper)
- **Peer benchmarks:** "你的竞品在小红书的投放效率是你的2倍" (Your competitor's Xiaohongshu ad efficiency is 2x yours)
- **Short video format:** 3-minute explanation videos outperform PDFs
- **WeChat article (公众号长文):** Still effective for detailed thought leadership

### Meeting Cadence and Style

- **First meeting:** 30 minutes max. Show ONE compelling data point, not a full deck.
- **Follow-up:** WeChat voice message or short text within 24 hours
- **POC proposal:** Must fit on one page with clear success metrics
- **Review cadence:** Weekly during pilot, monthly once established
- **Preferred time:** Afternoons (mornings consumed by platform data review)

## 10. Buying Triggers and Timing

### When CMOs Actively Buy

| Trigger | Signal | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| New platform policy | Algorithm change announcement | Within 2 weeks of announcement |
| Quarterly performance miss | Board pressure on CAC/ROI | End of quarter + 2 weeks |
| New product launch | Marketing plan being assembled | 6–8 weeks before launch |
| 双11/618 preparation | Major shopping festival prep | 2–3 months before event |
| Competitive threat | Competitor viral success | Immediately (reactive) |
| New fiscal year | Budget allocation | November–January (most companies) |
| CEO mandate | "We need to do 私域/直播/出海" | Unpredictable; move fast when it happens |

### Budget Cycles

- **Annual planning:** October–December for following year
- **Quarterly adjustment:** Real; 20–30% of budget can shift quarterly
- **Platform co-investment:** 抖音/腾讯 often co-fund campaigns (联合投入); timing aligns with platform fiscal needs
- **Pilot budgets:** ¥100K–500K typical for new vendor trial (3 months)
- **Full deployment:** ¥1M–10M+ annually for core MarTech

## 11. Objection Patterns

### Common Objections and What They Mean

| What They Say | What They Mean | How to Respond |
|---|---|---|
| "我们先内部讨论一下" (Let us discuss internally) | Could be genuine or polite rejection | Ask for specific timeline and next step |
| "效果怎么衡量?" (How do we measure results?) | They're interested but need ROI story for boss | Propose specific KPIs tied to their existing metrics |
| "我们现在用的还行" (Current solution is okay) | Switching cost feels high | Quantify opportunity cost of status quo |
| "预算已经分完了" (Budget already allocated) | Timing issue or price objection | Propose pilot from existing underperforming budget |
| "你们有没有同行案例?" (Do you have peer cases?) | Need social proof for internal selling | Provide 2–3 same-industry references |
| "能不能先免费试用?" (Can we try for free?) | Standard negotiation; also genuine risk aversion | Offer limited pilot with clear success criteria |
| "我们老板要看" (Boss needs to see) | Decision authority is above them | Prepare executive summary; offer to present to boss |

## 16. Anti-Patterns — What NOT to Do

### Guaranteed Ways to Lose This Persona

1. **Leading with Western case studies only.** "Coca-Cola used our platform to..." → Irrelevant. Show Chinese brand cases on Chinese platforms.

2. **Ignoring platform specificity.** Proposing a "channel-agnostic" solution when they live and die by 抖音 vs 小红书 vs 企微 specifics.

3. **Email-first outreach.** They won't see it. Period. Find a WeChat warm intro.

4. **Proposing 6-month implementation.** Their tenure might not last that long. Show value in 30 days or lose them.

5. **Talking about "brand awareness" without connecting to conversion.** In China's 品效合一 paradigm, pure brand play without ROI path is a hard sell except at the largest companies.

6. **Not understanding 个保法 implications.** If your solution requires data practices that violate PIPL, you're dead on arrival with legal review.

7. **Dismissing 直播/livestream.** Even if your product isn't directly related, acknowledge it as a core channel. It's not a gimmick — it's infrastructure.

8. **Sending long decks/PDFs.** They won't read them. Send a 2-minute video explanation or a 1-page WeChat article.

## 18. Working With Adjacent Personas

### CMO ↔ CTO/VP Engineering

- **Tension:** Marketing wants fast integration; engineering wants stable architecture
- **Common ground:** Both want data-driven decisions; shared interest in CDP/analytics
- **Key phrase:** "我们需要技术支撑营销创新" (We need tech to support marketing innovation)

### CMO ↔ CFO

- **Tension:** CFO wants lower spend; CMO wants more budget for growth
- **Common ground:** Both want efficient capital deployment; ROI language bridges
- **Key phrase:** "每一分钱都要算得清" (Every cent must be accountable)

### CMO ↔ CEO

- **Tension:** CEO has brand vision; CMO must execute within reality constraints
- **Common ground:** Both want market position improvement
- **Key phrase:** "老板,这是数据支撑的方案" (Boss, this proposal is data-supported)

### CMO ↔ Head of Sales

- **Tension:** Lead quality disputes; attribution fights
- **Common ground:** Both measured on revenue; pipeline is shared responsibility
- **Key phrase:** "市场和销售要拉通" (Marketing and sales must align/integrate)

## 19. Evolution and Trends (2025–2027)

### Role Evolution

1. **AI-native marketing:** AIGC (AI-generated content) is already producing 30–50% of performance ad creative. CMOs must become AI workflow managers.

2. **Video-first everything:** Text-based marketing (公众号 long articles) declining. Video号, 抖音, short-video native content is baseline.

3. **全域营销 (Omni-domain marketing):** Platforms offering unified public+private domain tools. CMO must orchestrate across domains.

4. **CGO role consolidation:** Growth + Brand continuing to merge. The "full-stack CMO" who can do both is increasingly valued.

5. **出海 becoming mandatory:** Even domestic-first companies now expected to have global brand strategy. CMO scope expanding internationally.

6. **AI agent marketing:** Early signals of autonomous marketing agents handling campaign optimization, content generation, and audience targeting. CMO role shifts from operator to strategist.

## 20. Summary: The Five Things to Remember

1. **Title matters less than function.** Look for 增长VP, 品牌VP, 市场VP — not just "CMO." The person controlling marketing budget may have unexpected titles.

2. **WeChat is infrastructure, not a channel.** 私域 strategy via 企业微信 is THE differentiator between Chinese and Western marketing. Any vendor must integrate.

3. **品效合一 is the paradigm.** Don't position as "brand only" or "performance only." Show how you bridge both. Every yuan must be attributable.

4. **Speed wins.** 30-day proof of value or you're replaced. 18-month average tenure means urgency is real, not manufactured.

5. **Platform-native trumps platform-agnostic.** These CMOs live inside 2–3 platforms. Deep integration with THEIR platforms beats broad but shallow coverage every time.
