## What a 出海VP Actually Is — China Context

This role **does not exist in Western companies**. A Western "VP International" expands an already-global company into new markets. A Chinese 出海VP must:

1. **Build from zero in hostile terrain** — Chinese brands have no inherent recognition abroad. Unlike Apple entering China (prestige pull), Anker entering the US had to earn trust from scratch against established brands.

2. **Navigate existential geopolitical risk** — TikTok faces forced divestiture. Shein's IPO was blocked. 海康威视 is on the Entity List. No Western exec faces the risk that their entire international business could be legislated out of existence overnight.

3. **Bridge two incompatible operating cultures** — China HQ operates with 高效执行 (hyper-execution), rapid iteration, 996-adjacent intensity. Overseas teams expect autonomy, work-life balance, local decision-making. The 出海VP must translate both directions.

4. **Maintain dual-stack everything** — Technology (domestic cloud vs AWS/GCP), legal entities (VIE structures, local incorporations), payment systems (支付宝/微信 vs Stripe/PayPal), marketing channels (抖音 vs TikTok, 小红书 vs Instagram).

5. **Own a P&L that's simultaneously high-growth and high-risk** — Overseas revenue is often the growth story for investors, but every market carries regulatory, currency, and political risk that domestic markets don't.

**Three waves of 出海 (critical context for understanding this role's evolution):**

| Wave | Period | Model | Exemplars | Outcome |
|------|--------|-------|-----------|---------|
| 工具出海 (Utility) | 2012–2016 | Free apps → ad monetization; volume over brand | 猎豹 Clean Master, APUS, UC Browser | Most collapsed when Google/Facebook changed policies |
| 品牌出海 (Brand) | 2016–2020 | Build real brands with product quality; DTC/marketplace | Anker, Shein (early), DJI | Winners built lasting brand equity |
| 生态出海 (Ecosystem) | 2020+ | Full platform/ecosystem export; local operations at scale | TikTok, Shein (mature), Temu, WeBull | Highest reward but highest geopolitical risk |

The 出海VP role crystallized during the second wave and became C-suite critical in the third wave. Today it is one of the most sought-after and highest-paid roles in Chinese tech.

## §1 Role Definition

### 1.1 Five 出海VP Archetypes

| Archetype | Description | Example Pattern |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **品牌出海型 (Brand Builder)** | Builds Chinese brand for overseas consumers from scratch; deep DTC/marketplace expertise; brand-first, channel-second | Anker's international team; early Shein brand positioning; Roborock global brand building |
| **平台扩张型 (Platform Expander)** | Takes a proven Chinese platform model and replicates it in new markets; heavy localization of product/ops | TikTok regional expansion; Temu market entry; WeBull US launch |
| **合规突围型 (Compliance Navigator)** | Operates in heavily regulated or sanctioned environments; primary skill is keeping business alive under legal/political pressure | 海康威视 post-Entity List; TikTok US operations; WeBull SEC/FINRA navigation |
| **并购整合型 (M&A Integrator)** | Grows international presence through acquisition; integrates foreign companies into Chinese parent | 海尔 GE Appliances integration; 腾讯 Riot Games / Supercell oversight; Midea acquiring KUKA |
| **区域深耕型 (Regional Deep-Diver)** | Focuses on specific high-growth regions (SEA, MENA, LatAm) rather than global breadth; builds local ecosystems | TikTok Southeast Asia commerce; Transsion (传音) Africa dominance; Shopee regional playbook |

### 1.2 Diagnostic Table — Identifying the Archetype

| Signal | Likely Archetype |
|--------|-----------------|
| Company sells branded products on Amazon/Shopify/DTC sites | 品牌出海型 |
| Company operates a consumer platform (social, commerce, fintech) | 平台扩张型 |
| Company is on Entity List or facing legislative threats in key markets | 合规突围型 |
| Company has acquired foreign businesses recently | 并购整合型 |
| Company revenue concentrated in 1–2 specific overseas regions | 区域深耕型 |
| Company is Shein/TikTok/Temu scale with multiple signals | Blend — 平台扩张型 primary + 合规突围型 secondary |

### 1.3 Three Time Horizons

| Horizon | Timeframe | Focus | Example |
|---------|-----------|-------|---------|
| **H1: Protect existing markets** | 0–6 months | Compliance, risk mitigation, existing market growth, team retention | TikTok managing US divestiture timeline; Shein preparing for EU Digital Services Act |
| **H2: Scale and optimize** | 6–18 months | New market entry, operational efficiency, local team building, brand building | Anker expanding into new EU categories; WeBull entering new APAC markets |
| **H3: Strategic positioning** | 18–36 months | New business models, ecosystem play, M&A, long-term regulatory strategy | 腾讯游戏 building Level Infinite as standalone global publisher; 海尔 smart home ecosystem export |

### 1.4 Four-Way Pull

The 出海VP is pulled in four directions simultaneously:

```
                    CEO / 创始人 / Board
                    "海外收入增长要50%+"
                    "投资人要看出海故事"
                         │
                         ▼
    China HQ ◄────── 出海VP ──────► Local Markets
    "按总部流程来"        │        "我们需要本地化决策权"
    "为什么海外团队        │        "中国产品在这里
     成本这么高?"         │         不能直接用"
                         │
                         ▼
              Geopolitics / Regulators
              "数据必须本地存储"
              "中国公司可能被禁"
              "需要本地法人实体"
```

**CEO/Board Pull** — Investors want the international growth story. Revenue targets are aggressive. The board asks every quarter: "When does overseas break even?"

**China HQ Pull** — Headquarters wants to maintain control. Product decisions, budgets, hiring approvals all flow through China. HQ doesn't understand why things take longer overseas. Cultural friction: "为什么海外团队不加班？"

**Local Markets Pull** — Each market demands localization. What works in SEA fails in MENA. Local teams want autonomy. Local hires don't understand HQ decision-making culture.

**Geopolitical/Regulatory Pull** — GDPR, CFIUS, DSA, local data residency, anti-China sentiment, forced divestiture threats. Each market has unique regulatory landmines.

## §2 Strategic Priorities

### Priority 1: Market Strategy — Where to Play

The most consequential decision a 出海VP makes: **which markets, in what sequence, with what model.**

**Market prioritization framework (as practiced by successful 出海 companies):**

| Market Tier | Markets | Opportunity | Risk Level | Strategy |
|-------------|---------|-------------|------------|----------|
| **Tier 1: Low risk, high growth** | Southeast Asia (东南亚): Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Philippines | Large population, mobile-first, relatively China-friendly | Low–Medium | Go early and deep; build local teams; acceptable as "China company" |
| **Tier 2: Medium risk, high growth** | Middle East (中东): Saudi, UAE; Latin America (拉美): Brazil, Mexico | Emerging digital economies, less anti-China sentiment | Medium | Partner-led entry; local JVs; adapt to cultural specifics |
| **Tier 3: High reward, high risk** | US, EU (especially UK, Germany, France) | Largest consumer markets; highest ARPU | High–Very High | Brand-first (hide China origin initially); heavy compliance investment; existential regulatory risk |
| **Tier 4: Strategic diversification** | Japan, Korea, Africa, South Asia | Specific opportunities but harder to scale | Varies | Opportunistic; often follow competitor moves |

**Key insight:** The best 出海VPs sequence markets deliberately. Anker perfected the model: **Amazon US first** (prove product-market fit on the world's most competitive marketplace) → **expand to EU** (similar model, larger addressable market) → **SEA/emerging** (brand cachet from Western success). Shein reversed this for fast fashion: **US/EU first** (high ARPU, trend-setting) → **global simultaneously**.

### Priority 2: Geopolitical Risk Management (THE Defining Challenge)

| Risk Type | Manifestation | Mitigation Pattern |
|-----------|---------------|--------------------|
| **Legislative ban** | TikTok forced divestiture; potential Temu restrictions | Singapore/Ireland HQ structure; local data storage; IP separation from China parent |
| **Entity List / sanctions** | 海康威视, 华为 | Diversify supply chain; build local R&D; create "clean" subsidiaries |
| **Investment screening (CFIUS/FDI)** | Blocking of Chinese acquisitions | Structure deals through third countries; minority stakes; operational control without ownership |
| **Data sovereignty** | GDPR, local data laws, CLOUD Act conflicts with China's 数据安全法 | Regional data centers; data architecture that physically separates China and overseas data |
| **Public sentiment / brand risk** | "Chinese spy app" narrative; Shein labor controversy | Local branding (deemphasize China origin); CSR investment; transparency reports |
| **Supply chain restrictions** | Tariffs, import bans, technology export controls | Manufacturing diversification (Vietnam, Mexico, India); local sourcing |

**Verbatim-style pain statement:** *"我们的核心风险不是竞争对手，而是某天早上醒来发现我们的产品被某个国家直接禁了。TikTok的教训我们都看在眼里。"*

**猎豹 cautionary tale:** In 2018–2019, 猎豹移动 had 80%+ overseas revenue from utility apps. Google terminated their AdMob account and removed apps from Play Store overnight. Revenue collapsed. The lesson every 出海VP has internalized: **platform dependency is existential risk.** This is why Anker built DTC capability, why Shein owns its own app distribution, and why TikTok fights divestiture — they cannot afford to be de-platformed.

### Priority 3: Localization vs. Globalization Tension (本地化 vs. 全球化)

The 出海VP constantly navigates what to standardize globally vs. what to localize per market:

| Dimension | Globalize (统一) | Localize (本地化) |
|-----------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Product core** | ✅ Core technology, algorithm, platform | ❌ |
| **UX/UI** | Base framework | ✅ Language, design, cultural norms |
| **Marketing** | Brand guidelines | ✅ Channels, creative, messaging, KOLs |
| **Operations** | Back-end systems | ✅ Customer service, logistics, payments |
| **Legal/Compliance** | IP strategy | ✅ Completely local per jurisdiction |
| **Team culture** | Company values (loosely) | ✅ Management style, working hours, compensation |
| **Pricing** | Strategy/positioning | ✅ Local pricing, currency, payment methods |

**The "Shein Playbook" (extreme globalization with surface localization):**
- Global: Supply chain, algorithm, app architecture, data infrastructure
- Local: Language, models/sizing, trend detection, payment, logistics last-mile, marketing creative

**The "海尔 Playbook" (deep localization through M&A):**
- Acquired GE Appliances → kept American brand, American leadership, American operations
- Acquired Fisher & Paykel (NZ), Candy (Italy) — each operates as quasi-independent brand
- Parent provides: capital, supply chain scale, R&D resources
- Local retains: brand identity, product design for local taste, go-to-market

### Priority 4: Organizational Design — The Hardest People Problem

| Model | Description | When to Use | Example |
|-------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **中央集权 (Centralized)** | HQ makes all decisions; overseas teams execute | Early-stage 出海; single market; speed matters | Early Shein (all decisions in Nanjing/Guangzhou) |
| **联邦制 (Federated)** | Regional autonomy with HQ coordination | Multi-region, mature markets, acquired brands | 海尔 (each brand operates independently); 腾讯游戏 (studios have autonomy) |
| **矩阵式 (Matrix)** | Functional reporting (to HQ) + regional reporting | Scaling phase; need both global efficiency and local responsiveness | Anker (functional COEs + regional teams); ByteDance/TikTok |
| **海外总部 (Overseas HQ)** | Create a non-China HQ for international (often Singapore) | Geopolitical risk mitigation; attract non-Chinese talent | Shein (Singapore); ByteDance (Singapore for TikTok) |

**Key tension:** China HQ wants control (high execution culture, fast iteration). Overseas teams want autonomy (local expertise, different pace). The 出海VP is the bridge.

**Common failure mode:** HQ imposes China-speed expectations on overseas markets. Example: "为什么欧洲团队周末不回消息?" → talent exodus → market performance decline → blame the 出海VP.

### Priority 5: Cross-Border Technology Architecture

| Challenge | Solution Pattern | Example |
|-----------|------------------|---------|
| Data residency (China data can't leave; EU data must stay in EU) | Regional data planes; separate deployments per jurisdiction | ByteDance Project Texas; Shein multi-region architecture |
| China ↔ Overseas network reliability | Dedicated cross-border links; CDN strategy; edge computing | All 出海 companies struggle with China firewall latency |
| Payment systems | Multi-PSP integration; local wallets per market | Shein integrating 50+ payment methods globally |
| Cloud strategy | China: 阿里云/腾讯云/华为云; Overseas: AWS/GCP/Azure | Dual-stack with shared CI/CD and separate data planes |
| App distribution | China: 应用宝/华为/小米 stores; Overseas: Apple App Store + Google Play | Separate builds, separate review processes |
| Compliance automation | Per-jurisdiction compliance engines; automated reporting | WeBull: SEC/FINRA reporting automation; Shein: EU DSA compliance |

### Priority 6: Overseas Talent Strategy

| Challenge | 出海VP Approach |
|-----------|----------------|
| Hiring senior local talent who trust a Chinese company | Competitive comp (often above-market); autonomy promises; equity if pre-IPO |
| 海归 talent (returning overseas Chinese) | Bridge roles; understand both cultures; but sometimes lack deep local networks |
| China expats (外派) | Useful for knowledge transfer but burn out fast; often culturally mismatched |
| Local talent retention after media crises | Proactive communication; demonstrate separation from China politics; emphasize product mission |
| Cultural friction between HQ and field | 出海VP as translator; structured communication cadences; clear decision-rights frameworks |

**Talent market insight:** After TikTok's US struggles, many Chinese 出海 companies report increased difficulty hiring American/European senior talent. The 出海VP must actively counternarrate and offer structural protections (e.g., local board, data isolation) to attract top local hires.

## §4 Pain Points

### 4.1 Geopolitical Existential Risk

| Pain | Manifestation |
|------|---------------|
| "We could be banned tomorrow" | TikTok precedent; RESTRICT Act; EU gatekeeper designation; India app bans (2020) |
| No playbook for unprecedented risks | Lawyers can't predict political outcomes; scenario planning is the only tool |
| Brand contamination | "Chinese company" label triggers distrust regardless of product quality |
| Supply chain weaponization | Tariffs change overnight; shipping routes disrupted; component access restricted |
| Data localization arms race | Every country wants data on their soil; multiplies infrastructure cost |

**Verbatim-style pain statement:** *"我们不是怕竞争对手，怕的是政策。TikTok几百亿美元的业务，说要卖就要卖。我们规模小一点就更脆弱了。"*

### 4.2 HQ-Field Culture Clash

| Pain | Manifestation |
|------|---------------|
| HQ demands China-speed execution overseas | "为什么美国团队周五下午就不回复了?" → deep cultural misunderstanding |
| Decision authority unclear | Local team proposes plan → HQ overrides without context → local team demotivated |
| Communication timezone hell | Critical decisions need both sides awake; 12-hour gaps between US and China |
| "Translated" vs. truly localized product | HQ ships Chinese product with English text; calls it "出海" — customers reject |
| Cost perception | HQ sees overseas employee costs (3–5x China salary) as excessive → underfunds |

**Verbatim-style pain statement:** *"总部觉得招一个美国工程师要100万人民币太贵了。但在硅谷这是入门价。如果不付市场价，只能招到二流人才，然后出二流结果。"*

### 4.3 Multi-Market Complexity

| Pain | Manifestation |
|------|---------------|
| Every market is different | What works in Indonesia fails in Saudi Arabia; can't simply replicate |
| Compliance multiplication | GDPR + CCPA + LGPD + PDPA + ... each with different requirements |
| Currency/payment fragmentation | 50+ payment methods across markets; each with integration cost |
| Logistics nightmare | Cross-border shipping, returns, duties, warehousing — per market |
| Local competition | Not just competing with other Chinese 出海 companies but entrenched local players |

### 4.4 Talent Challenges

| Pain | Manifestation |
|------|---------------|
| Hard to recruit senior Western talent | "Why would I work for a Chinese company I've never heard of?" |
| Expatriate burnout | Chinese expats (外派) overwork, culture shock, family separation → 2-year max |
| Bridge talent scarcity | People who deeply understand BOTH China HQ culture AND Western markets are rare and expensive (¥3–8M packages) |
| Local leader retention after controversy | When Shein gets bad press, local team members question their career choice |
| 35岁 HQ-side worry | HQ-based 出海 staff fear being seen as "not core" to domestic business |

### 4.5 Profitability Pressure

| Pain | Manifestation |
|------|---------------|
| International expansion is expensive | Market entry cost: $10–50M per major market before breakeven |
| Long payback periods | Board/investors want growth but also demand path to profitability |
| FX risk | Revenue in local currencies; reporting in RMB; margin volatility |
| Duplicate infrastructure cost | Maintaining parallel systems (China + overseas) doubles OpEx |
| Marketing cost inflation | Meta/Google CPMs rising; brand-building in new markets requires sustained spend |

### 4.6 Platform Dependency Risk (猎豹 Syndrome)

| Pain | Manifestation |
|------|---------------|
| Google/Apple distribution power | One policy change can delist your app (猎豹 lost all Google distribution) |
| Amazon algorithm/policy changes | Anker had listings suspended during Amazon purge of review manipulation |
| Meta/Google ad policy | Ad accounts suspended without warning; appeal processes opaque |
| Payment processor risk | Stripe/PayPal can freeze accounts if flagged; no recourse timeline |
| AWS/GCP infrastructure dependency | If US sanctions escalate, cloud provider could terminate service |

**Key lesson from 猎豹:** In 2019, Google removed 猎豹's apps from Play Store and terminated their AdMob account. Overnight, a $600M+ revenue business collapsed. Every 出海VP now asks: "What's our 猎豹 scenario and how do we survive it?"

## §5 Objection Patterns

### Objection 1: "我们海外业务还太小，不需要这么复杂的方案" (Our overseas business is too small for this)

**Why they say it:**
- Genuinely early-stage; overseas revenue <10% of total
- Budget constrained for overseas vs. domestic priorities
- "Let's prove the market first, then invest in infrastructure"

**Frequency:** 60%+ for companies in early 出海 stage

**Counter-framework:**

| Angle | Approach |
|-------|----------|
| **Prevention > cure** | "TikTok and Shein wish they'd built compliance infrastructure earlier. Retrofitting is 5x more expensive than building correctly from day one" |
| **Scale-ready architecture** | "The cost difference between a solution that handles 1 market vs. 10 markets is minimal. Build for scale now" |
| **猎豹 example** | "猎豹 was generating $600M overseas and still got wiped out because they didn't invest in risk diversification" |
| **Investor narrative** | "Your investors are asking about 出海 strategy. Having proper infrastructure signals maturity and de-risks the story" |

### Objection 2: "总部产品直接翻译到海外就行" (Just translate HQ's product for overseas)

**Why they say it:**
- HQ product team doesn't want to build separate overseas version
- Cost and complexity of true localization seems excessive
- "Our product is already good, just needs translation"

**Counter-framework:**

| Angle | Approach |
|-------|----------|
| **Failure examples** | "This is exactly what 猎豹 did — Chinese utility apps with English text. No brand loyalty, no switching cost, easily displaced" |
| **Data proof** | "Shein's success comes from true localization: different trends per market, local models, local sizing. Their conversion is 3x apps that just translate" |
| **User research** | "Let us show you user research from [target market]. What Chinese users value is fundamentally different from what [market] users value" |
| **Revenue impact** | "Companies with deep localization see 40–60% higher retention vs. translated-only products in the same category" |

### Objection 3: "地缘政治风险我们控制不了，做好产品就行" (We can't control geopolitics, just make good product)

**Why they say it:**
- Fatalism — "if they want to ban us, they will anyway"
- Engineering/product mindset — "focus on what we can control"
- Underestimation of risk — "we're not as high-profile as TikTok"

**Counter-framework:**

| Angle | Approach |
|-------|----------|
| **Structural mitigation** | "You can't control politics but you CAN control corporate structure, data architecture, and narrative. Singapore HQ, local data storage, transparent governance — these make you harder to target" |
| **Entity List lesson** | "海康威视 didn't expect to be sanctioned either. The companies that prepared survived; those that didn't had to scramble" |
| **Investor requirement** | "In 2025, institutional investors require geopolitical risk assessment for any China 出海 investment. It's table stakes for fundraising" |
| **Insurance analogy** | "You don't control whether there's a fire. You still buy fire insurance. Geopolitical risk architecture is your insurance" |

### Objection 4: "海外团队太贵了，能不能用国内团队远程管" (Overseas teams are too expensive, can we manage remotely from China?)

**Why they say it:**
- Genuine sticker shock — US/EU staff cost 3–5x China equivalent
- HQ finance pushes back on overseas headcount costs
- "We have excellent people in China who speak English"

**Counter-framework:**

| Angle | Approach |
|-------|----------|
| **Timezone reality** | "A 12-hour timezone gap means decisions take 2 days instead of 2 hours. Multiply by 100 decisions/month — you lose months of velocity" |
| **Regulatory requirement** | "GDPR requires a local DPO. SEC requires local compliance officers. Some roles legally must be local" |
| **Customer trust** | "B2B customers in the US won't buy from a team that's unavailable during their business hours. Consumer brands need local understanding" |
| **Total cost of failure** | "You save $500K/year on local team but lose $10M in market opportunity because you can't move at local speed" |

### Objection 5: "我们先做东南亚，欧美风险太大" (Let's just do SEA, US/EU is too risky)

**Why they say it:**
- Legitimate risk aversion after TikTok/Shein headlines
- SEA is easier (less anti-China sentiment, cultural proximity)
- Board is nervous about US/EU regulatory exposure

**Counter-framework:**

| Angle | Approach |
|-------|----------|
| **Acknowledge and validate** | "SEA-first is exactly Anker's original playbook and it works. But the question is: what's your 3-year plan?" |
| **ARPU reality** | "US/EU ARPU is 5–10x SEA. A smaller user base in US generates more revenue. Risk-adjusted, it may still be the best bet" |
| **Brand halo effect** | "Winning in US/EU gives you brand credibility everywhere else. Anker's Amazon #1 status makes SEA easy. Not vice versa" |
| **Portfolio approach** | "You don't have to choose. The right architecture supports multi-market from day one. De-risk by diversifying across 3+ regions simultaneously" |


### 6.6 Vendor Selection Criteria (Ranked)

1. **Multi-market capability** — Can it work across all our target markets from one platform?
2. **China-company friendly** — Will they work with a Chinese parent? (some vendors won't post-2022)
3. **Compliance built-in** — Does it help us meet GDPR/local regulations automatically?
4. **Speed to deploy** — We need to be live in a new market in weeks, not months
5. **Scalability** — Can handle 10x growth as we scale
6. **Local expertise** — Do they understand the specific markets we're targeting?
7. **Integration with China-side systems** — Can it connect to our domestic ERP/CRM/data systems?
8. **Data residency options** — Can data stay in-region? Can we prove separation from China?
9. **Cost at scale** — What's the 3-year TCO as we grow?
10. **Reference customers** — Other Chinese 出海 companies successfully using it?

## Appendix C: 出海 Three-Wave Evolution Detail

Understanding the three waves is critical context for engaging any 出海VP:

### Wave 1: 工具出海 (2012–2016)

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Model** | Free mobile utility apps (cleaners, browsers, launchers) → monetize via ads |
| **Exemplars** | 猎豹 (Clean Master, CM Security), APUS, UC Browser (Alibaba), SHAREit |
| **Success factors** | Google Play ASO, Facebook Ads arbitrage, cheap installs in emerging markets |
| **Revenue scale** | 猎豹 peak: ~$600M overseas revenue; most others $50–200M |
| **Why it collapsed** | Platform dependency (Google changed policies); no brand loyalty; easily replaced; zero switching cost |
| **Legacy lesson for today's 出海VP** | "Never depend on a single distribution platform. Build owned channels. Build brand." |

### Wave 2: 品牌出海 (2016–2020)

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Model** | Build real overseas brands with product differentiation; own the customer relationship |
| **Exemplars** | Anker (electronics), DJI (drones), Roborock (vacuums), early Shein |
| **Success factors** | Product quality, Amazon marketplace mastery, building brand through reviews/word-of-mouth |
| **Revenue scale** | Anker: ~¥20B+ total revenue (80%+ overseas); DJI: dominant global share |
| **Key innovation** | Chinese supply chain cost advantage + Western brand-building = unbeatable value proposition |
| **Legacy lesson** | "Brand equity is the ultimate moat. Once you're 'the brand,' platforms need you more than you need them" |

### Wave 3: 生态出海 (2020–present)

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Model** | Export entire platform/ecosystem; build local operations at massive scale; billions in revenue |
| **Exemplars** | TikTok (social), Shein (fast fashion), Temu (marketplace), WeBull (fintech), Genshin Impact (gaming) |
| **Success factors** | Massive capital deployment, algorithm advantage, hyper-localized operations, speed of execution |
| **Revenue scale** | TikTok: ~$20B+ global; Shein: ~$30B+; Temu: $15B+ (2024 estimates) |
| **Key risk** | Geopolitical backlash; regulatory targeting specifically because of scale and Chinese origin |
| **Current challenge** | Proving to Western governments that "Chinese platform ≠ security threat" |
