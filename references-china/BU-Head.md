## What a BU Head Actually Is — China Context

In Western companies, a "Business Unit Head" or "Division President" is a senior leader who manages a P&L but operates within strong corporate governance — shared services, centralized IT, matrix-reported functions. In China's technology landscape, the BU Head (事业群总裁) is something **fundamentally different**:

1. **内部CEO (Internal CEO)** — The BU Head has P&L ownership, independent hiring authority, often an independent tech stack, and sometimes even a separate physical office location. They function as a CEO of a company-within-a-company.

2. **赛马机制参赛者 (Horse-Race Competitor)** — Multiple BUs compete for corporate resources, headcount, strategic priority, and CEO attention. Winning BUs grow; losing BUs get absorbed, dissolved, or starved. This is Darwinian, not collaborative.

3. **The Real Enterprise Buyer** — For vendors selling to large Chinese tech companies, the BU Head is often the actual decision-maker, NOT the corporate CTO or CIO. Each BU has its own budget, its own vendor relationships, and its own procurement authority.

4. **Political Operator** — The BU Head must simultaneously deliver results AND manage upward (CEO relationship), laterally (competing BU heads), and downward (retaining talent who have offers from other BUs or external companies).

5. **CEO Succession Candidate** — The top-performing BU Head is frequently the next CEO. This role is the proving ground for group-level leadership.

**Why this role is China-unique:**

The 事业群/事业部 structure is THE dominant organizational model for large Chinese tech companies (ByteDance, Tencent, Alibaba, Meituan, Hikvision, Haier). It evolved from:
- Need to maintain startup agility at massive scale
- Founder preference for internal competition over consensus
- Chinese management philosophy that rewards individual accountability over collective governance
- Practical need to give ambitious executives "their own kingdom" to prevent departure

## §1 Role Definition

### 1.1 Six BU Head Archetypes

| Archetype | Description | Example Pattern |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **赛马冠军型 (Horse-Race Champion)** | Won internal competition to lead the dominant BU; hyper-growth oriented, politically astute | ByteDance 抖音负责人 growing from experiment to core revenue; Tencent 微信事业群 eclipsing QQ |
| **产业开拓型 (Industry Pioneer)** | Builds new business lines from zero; startup-within-corporation mentality | ByteDance 火山引擎 (cloud/enterprise); 海康威视 创新业务; Alibaba 本地生活 |
| **稳态经营型 (Steady-State Operator)** | Manages mature, profitable BU; focuses on efficiency, margin, and incremental growth | Hikvision PBG (公共服务); Tencent PCG after restructuring; established industrial BUs |
| **工作室群主 (Studio Group Master)** | Creative/product-driven leader managing portfolio of semi-autonomous teams | 腾讯天美工作室群; 光子工作室群; game/content studio clusters |
| **平台赋能型 (Platform Enabler)** | Runs internal platform BU that serves other BUs and external customers | ByteDance 飞书; Alibaba 阿里云; Haier COSMOPlat; 海康萤石 |
| **人单合一型 (RenDanHeYi Entrepreneur)** | Extreme decentralization model; effectively runs a micro-enterprise within the group | 海尔 产业线/小微 leaders; companies adopting Haier's 人单合一 management innovation |

### 1.2 Diagnostic Table — Identifying the Archetype

| Signal | Likely Archetype |
|--------|-----------------|
| BU is fastest-growing division; frequent media coverage | 赛马冠军型 |
| BU was founded <3 years ago; exploring new TAM | 产业开拓型 |
| BU generates 40%+ of group profit; stable growth | 稳态经营型 |
| BU contains multiple named "studios" or "labs" | 工作室群主 |
| BU sells to other internal BUs AND external customers | 平台赋能型 |
| BU operates with independent brand, separate legal entity | 人单合一型 |
| Multiple signals present | Blend — lead with dominant, acknowledge secondary |

### 1.3 Three Time Horizons

| Horizon | Timeframe | Focus | Example |
|---------|-----------|-------|---------|
| **H1: Deliver the quarter** | 0–6 months | Revenue target, user growth, product launches, talent retention | 抖音 hitting DAU/monetization targets; Hikvision EBG hitting quarterly revenue |
| **H2: Competitive positioning** | 6–18 months | Market share expansion, new product lines, organizational scaling | 火山引擎 building enterprise customer base; 天美 launching next flagship title |
| **H3: Strategic transformation** | 18–36 months | New business models, international expansion, AI/platform bets | ByteDance BUs integrating 豆包 AI; Hikvision BUs transitioning from hardware to AIoT services |

### 1.4 The Five-Way Pull

The China BU Head is pulled in five directions simultaneously:

```
                    CEO / 集团总裁
                    "你的BU下季度能不能翻倍?"
                    "集团战略方向变了，你得配合"
                         │
                         ▼
   Competing BUs ◄──── BU Head ────► Customers / Market
   "他们在抢我的人"        │           "客户要整体解决方案
    "资源应该给我们"       │            但那是另一个BU的地盘"
    "他们的产品跟          │           "竞品已经发布了"
     我们重叠"            │
                         │
              ┌──────────┼──────────┐
              ▼                      ▼
    Internal Team              Group Functions
    "我们人手不够"           "集团IT要统一平台"
    "竞品开更高薪"           "财务要合规审计"
    "方向老变我们             "HR说编制冻结了"
     很疲惫"
```

**CEO/Group Pull** — The CEO sets direction, allocates resources, and can restructure BUs overnight. A single meeting can grow or kill a BU. The BU Head must maintain CEO confidence while managing expectations.

**Competing BU Pull** — Other BU Heads compete for the same pool of headcount, server resources, strategic priority, and CEO attention. Product boundaries overlap. Talent poaching between BUs is common.

**Market Pull** — Customers want integrated solutions that may span multiple BUs. Competitors (external) move fast. Missing a market window is existential for a new BU.

**Team Pull** — BU employees face burnout, competing offers (both internal and external), and direction fatigue from frequent pivots. Key talent is the BU's lifeblood.

**Group Functions Pull** — Corporate IT, Finance, HR, and Legal push for standardization that constrains BU autonomy. The BU Head's relationship with group functions is often adversarial.

## §2 Strategic Priorities

### Priority 1: Revenue Growth & P&L Ownership

The BU Head's #1 job is delivering the P&L. Unlike Western division heads who may share P&L with corporate, the Chinese BU Head has **unambiguous ownership**.

**Key dynamics:**

| Dimension | Reality |
|-----------|---------|
| Revenue target | Set in annual/quarterly planning with CEO; often aggressive (30–100% YoY for growth BUs) |
| Cost control | Full authority over BU spending; headcount is the largest lever |
| Profit expectation | New BUs: growth over profit (GMV/DAU focus). Mature BUs: margin expansion demanded |
| Internal transfer pricing | Platform BUs must price competitively vs. external or lose internal customers |
| Subsidy from group | New BUs may receive "战略投入" (strategic investment) for 2–3 years; then must self-sustain |

**What success looks like by archetype:**
- 赛马冠军型: Revenue exceeds all other BUs; becomes "核心业务" (core business)
- 产业开拓型: Achieves product-market fit; converts from cost center to revenue contributor
- 稳态经营型: Margin expansion quarter-over-quarter; no major customer churn
- 工作室群主: Hit titles that generate ¥1B+ annual revenue; maintain pipeline
- 平台赋能型: Growing external revenue as % of total (proving market competitiveness)

### Priority 2: Talent War (Internal and External)

BU Heads fight a two-front talent war:

**External competition:**
- BAT/ByteDance/Meituan all compete for the same engineers, product managers, and sales talent
- AI talent is especially scarce; a senior ML engineer has 5+ competing offers at any time
- Compensation packages: base + equity + BU-specific bonuses create complex negotiations

**Internal competition (unique to China BU structure):**
- Other BUs within the same company actively recruit your best people
- A star engineer can transfer to a "hotter" BU with better equity upside
- BU Head must create narrative: "This BU is the future; staying here means more growth"
- Headcount allocation is zero-sum — 200 new headcount split among 6 BUs means political negotiation

**Talent retention levers:**
| Lever | How Used |
|-------|----------|
| Equity/options tied to BU performance | Align individual upside with BU success |
| Title inflation | Promote generously within BU (Director = Manager elsewhere) |
| Project ownership | Give senior ICs ownership of visible, high-impact projects |
| BU culture | Create distinct identity — 天美's "champion culture"; 飞书's "product geek" culture |
| CEO access | Arrange skip-level meetings with group CEO for top performers |

### Priority 3: 赛马机制 Navigation (Internal Horse Racing)

The 赛马机制 is the defining dynamic of Chinese BU leadership. Multiple teams/BUs work on overlapping products; the winner gets resources, the loser gets dissolved.

**Famous examples:**
- Tencent: 微信 vs. 手机QQ for mobile messaging (微信 won → became core BU)
- ByteDance: Multiple short-video products competed; 抖音 won → subsumed others
- Alibaba: 淘宝 vs. 天猫 vs. 拼多多-response products — constant restructuring

**How BU Heads navigate the horse race:**

| Strategy | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **速度取胜 (Speed wins)** | Ship faster than competing BU; first-mover gets user/customer lock-in |
| **数据说话 (Let data speak)** | Prepare dashboards showing superior metrics for CEO review |
| **占领山头 (Claim the hill)** | Quickly establish the BU as the "owner" of a strategic area before others can contest |
| **结盟 (Alliance building)** | Form alliances with other BU heads against common internal threats |
| **向上管理 (Manage up)** | Ensure CEO hears your narrative first; frame competitors' results negatively |
| **人才虹吸 (Talent siphon)** | Attract the best people from weaker BUs, creating a flywheel |

**What happens when you lose the horse race:**
- Best case: BU absorbed into winner; you become VP under the winning BU Head
- Common case: BU dissolved; team dispersed; you leave the company
- Worst case: BU dissolved AND public narrative that you "failed" — career damage

### Priority 4: Product & Technology Strategy

The BU Head owns product direction and technology choices independently:

| Decision | BU Head Authority |
|----------|-------------------|
| Product roadmap | Full ownership; no corporate product committee override |
| Tech stack selection | Can choose independently (even if different from other BUs) |
| Build vs. buy | Decides whether to use group platform or external vendors |
| AI integration | Determines how/when to integrate 大模型 capabilities |
| Data ownership | Controls BU data; may resist sharing with other BUs |

**The technology independence tension:**
- Group CTO wants unified infrastructure (cost efficiency, security, standards)
- BU Head wants freedom to move fast with best-fit technology
- Resolution: Usually BU Head wins for customer-facing tech; group wins for back-office/infra
- Exception: Mandatory security/compliance requirements override BU autonomy

### Priority 5: Organizational Scaling & Structure

As BUs grow, the BU Head must evolve the organization:

| BU Stage | Headcount | Structure | BU Head Focus |
|----------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| Startup | 50–200 | Flat, everyone reports up | Hands-on product/tech decisions |
| Growth | 200–1000 | Functional teams form | Hiring VPs, delegation, process |
| Scale | 1000–5000 | Sub-BUs or departments | Strategy, resource allocation, politics |
| Mature | 5000+ | Multiple layers, P&L sub-units | Portfolio management, succession planning |

**China-specific organizational patterns:**
- 中台 (Middle Platform): Some BUs build their own mini-中台 for shared capabilities
- 前台小团队 (Front-end small teams): Customer-facing rapid-response squads
- 业务BP (Business Partners): Embedding HR/Finance into BU for faster decisions
- 虚拟组织 (Virtual orgs): Cross-BU project teams that the BU Head may sponsor or resist

### Priority 6: External Market & Customer Relationships

For B2B-oriented BUs (Hikvision BGs, ByteDance 火山引擎, enterprise divisions):

| Dimension | BU Head Role |
|-----------|--------------|
| Key accounts | Personally manages top 5–10 strategic customer relationships |
| Channel strategy | Decides direct vs. partner vs. hybrid go-to-market |
| Pricing authority | Sets pricing strategy; can approve large deal discounts |
| Industry focus | Chooses which verticals to prioritize (government, finance, manufacturing, etc.) |
| Competitive positioning | Defines how BU differentiates vs. external competitors |

For B2C-oriented BUs (抖音, 腾讯游戏 studios, consumer products):

| Dimension | BU Head Role |
|-----------|--------------|
| User growth | DAU/MAU targets are existential metrics |
| Monetization | Balances user experience vs. revenue extraction |
| Content ecosystem | Manages creator/developer relationships |
| Regulatory compliance | 防沉迷, content moderation, data privacy |
| International expansion | 出海 strategy for the BU's products |

## §4 Mental Model & Decision Framework

### 4.1 How the BU Head Thinks

**Core mental model: "I am running a company within a company"**

The BU Head thinks like a CEO but with unique constraints:
- Has a "board" of one (the actual CEO) who can restructure everything overnight
- Has "competitors" who sit in the same building (other BU Heads)
- Has "investors" (corporate capital allocation) who compare returns across BUs quarterly
- Has "regulatory risk" from both external government AND internal group policies

**Decision-making speed:**
Chinese BU Heads decide faster than Western equivalents because:
- No committee culture — the BU Head IS the committee
- Flat authority structure within BU — word of BU Head = law
- Competitive pressure demands speed — hesitation means another BU or external competitor wins
- CEO expectation of rapid execution — "I gave you the direction Monday; show me results Friday"

### 4.2 Decision Framework

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         BU HEAD DECISION MATRIX                  │
├────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┤
│ Decision Type  │ Process                         │
├────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┤
│ <¥5M spend     │ BU Head decides alone           │
│ ¥5M–¥50M      │ BU Head + BU Finance review     │
│ >¥50M          │ Requires CEO/Group approval     │
│ Hiring <50 HC  │ BU Head approves directly       │
│ Hiring >50 HC  │ Requires Group HR sign-off      │
│ New product    │ BU Head launches; informs CEO   │
│ Kill product   │ BU Head decides; informs CEO    │
│ Enter new mkt  │ BU Head proposes; CEO approves  │
│ M&A/investment │ Always escalates to Group       │
│ Cross-BU collab│ Peer negotiation; CEO arbitrates│
└────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 4.3 What Keeps the BU Head Up at Night

| Fear | Why It's Existential |
|------|---------------------|
| CEO reorganization | One meeting can merge/dissolve your BU; you have no veto |
| Internal competitor BU growing faster | Signals your BU is losing the horse race |
| Key talent departing to competitor BU | Talent loss creates vicious cycle |
| Missing quarterly targets 2x in a row | CEO confidence erodes exponentially |
| External competitor disruption | Validates narrative that "this BU should be restructured" |
| Group function power grab | Corporate IT/HR standardization that removes your autonomy |
| Product-market shift | Your BU's core value proposition becomes obsolete |

### 4.4 Vendor/Partner Evaluation Lens

When a BU Head evaluates external vendors or partners:

| Criterion | Weight | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Speed to value | Very High | Must show results within 1 quarter to maintain momentum |
| BU-specific customization | High | Generic "enterprise" solutions don't fit; needs BU-specific integration |
| Independence from group | Medium-High | Prefers vendors not already locked to competing BU or group functions |
| Reference customers in same vertical | High | Peer proof within the specific industry segment |
| Pricing flexibility | Medium | Has budget authority but is cost-conscious about ROI |
| No corporate escalation required | High | Strongly prefers decisions that stay within BU authority |
| Chinese vendor preference | Medium | Pragmatic — best solution wins, but 信创 awareness |
| Long-term partnership potential | Medium | Values vendors who grow with the BU's trajectory |

## §5 Communication Style & Engagement

### 5.1 Language Patterns

**In CEO reviews (quarterly business review / QBR):**
- "本季度BU收入增长47%，超额完成目标" (This quarter BU revenue grew 47%, exceeding target)
- "我们在XX赛道已经是行业第一" (We are already #1 in XX segment)
- "需要集团在AI算力上给我们更多支持" (Need more group support on AI compute resources)
- "竞品在做XX，我们的应对方案是..." (Competitors are doing XX, our response is...)

**In BU all-hands:**
- "我们是一家独立的公司，要有创业心态" (We are an independent company; maintain startup mentality)
- "不要等集团的决定，我们自己先干起来" (Don't wait for group decisions; we start ourselves)
- "这个季度是生死之战" (This quarter is a life-or-death battle)
- "我给大家争取到了XX资源" (I've secured XX resources for us)

**To vendors/partners:**
- "我不需要跟集团汇报这个决定" (I don't need to report this decision to the group)
- "能不能下个月就上线？我们等不了三个月" (Can we go live next month? We can't wait three months)
- "给我看同行业的案例" (Show me cases in the same industry)
- "如果效果好，我可以追加预算" (If results are good, I can increase the budget)

### 5.2 Meeting Preferences

| Meeting Type | Frequency | Duration | BU Head Expectation |
|-------------|-----------|----------|---------------------|
| CEO 1:1 | Weekly–Biweekly | 30–60 min | Update on results, request resources, flag risks |
| BU leadership team | Weekly | 60–90 min | Fast decisions, accountability review |
| Product review | Biweekly | 2–3 hours | Deep dive on product metrics, roadmap adjustments |
| All-hands | Monthly–Quarterly | 60 min | Motivation, direction-setting, celebrating wins |
| Vendor evaluation | As needed | 30–45 min | Decision-focused; hates "introductory" meetings |
| Cross-BU coordination | Rare, reluctant | 30 min max | Only when CEO mandates or mutual benefit is obvious |

### 5.3 How to Get a Meeting with a BU Head

| Channel | Effectiveness | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Through BU's own team (VP-level referral) | Highest | BU Head trusts their own people's judgment |
| Direct WeChat from peer BU Head | High | Peer respect matters |
| Through CEO/Group office | Medium-High | Works but signals "you needed escalation" |
| Cold outreach via corporate | Low | Gets filtered by corporate gatekeepers who don't represent BU |
| Industry event encounter | Medium | BU Heads attend events in their specific vertical |
| Through key customer reference | High | "Your customer XX recommended you talk to us" |

### 5.4 Relationship Dynamics

**With CEO:**
- Manages up carefully — must project confidence while being honest about risks
- Knows that CEO relationships with other BU Heads affect resource allocation
- Understands that CEO may have already decided the strategic direction; role is to execute
- The 1:1 with CEO is the most important meeting of the week

**With peer BU Heads:**
- Mix of collaboration and competition (co-opetition)
- Will collaborate when both benefit (shared customer, complementary products)
- Will compete fiercely when boundaries overlap
- WeChat group dynamics: What you say in the group vs. what you say 1:1 to CEO differ

**With Group Functions (Corporate IT, HR, Finance):**
- Relationship is often adversarial
- BU Head views group functions as constraints on autonomy
- "集团IT要统一系统" = "They're trying to slow us down"
- Will comply minimally with corporate mandates while maximizing BU independence
- Exception: Group Legal/Compliance — must comply due to regulatory risk

**With BU Team:**
- Expected to be decisive, present, and personally accountable
- "护犊子" (protecting your people) is valued — fight for your team's interests externally
- Must balance pushing hard on targets with not burning out the team
- Star performers get direct access; underperformers get replaced quickly

## §6 Buying Behavior & Sales Engagement

### 6.1 Budget & Procurement Authority

| Item | BU Head Authority |
|------|-------------------|
| SaaS subscriptions | Full authority up to ¥5M–¥10M/year |
| Professional services | Full authority up to ¥10M–¥20M |
| Hardware/infrastructure | Full authority up to ¥20M–¥50M |
| Strategic partnerships | Can sign LOI; formal contract may need Group Legal review |
| Multi-year commitments (>3yr) | Usually requires Group CFO awareness |
| M&A/investments | Always escalates to Group |

**Key insight for sellers:** The BU Head can say "yes" to most enterprise deals without corporate approval. This makes them a faster, more decisive buyer than corporate CXOs — but also means you must sell to BU-specific value, not "enterprise-wide" stories.

### 6.2 What Triggers a Purchase Decision

| Trigger | Example | Urgency Level |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| Competitive threat | "竞品BU已经用了这个技术" | Critical — days to decide |
| CEO mandate | "集团要求所有BU接入大模型" | High — weeks to execute |
| Quarterly target gap | "This quarter's revenue is 20% behind" | High — needs immediate lever |
| Talent competitive offer | "Competitor offers better tooling to engineers" | Medium-High |
| Customer demand | "Top 3 customers are asking for this feature" | High |
| Cost optimization pressure | "Need to cut 15% costs without cutting headcount" | Medium |
| New market entry | "Entering XX vertical; need domain-specific capabilities" | Medium |

### 6.3 Sales Process with a BU Head

| Stage | BU Head Behavior | Seller Strategy |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Awareness | Hears about solution from BU team member or peer | Seed through BU's own technical/product leaders first |
| Interest | Asks: "这个能帮我们快速见效吗?" (Can this show quick results?) | Lead with speed-to-value; show 30-day implementation path |
| Evaluation | Delegates to BU VP; asks for competitive comparison | Support the BU VP with detailed technical proof + ROI model |
| Decision | 30-min meeting; decides based on: speed, BU-fit, peer reference | In the meeting: 3 slides max. Problem → Solution → Timeline → Price |
| Negotiation | Pushes on price AND timeline; may ask for BU-exclusive terms | Be flexible on terms that don't cost much; firm on timeline commitments |
| Expansion | If results good, expands quickly and becomes internal champion | Deliver outstanding results in first 90 days; BU Head will refer to peers |

### 6.4 Deal Killers

| Deal Killer | Why |
|-------------|-----|
| "Need to align with your corporate IT first" | Implies BU Head doesn't have authority; insulting |
| Implementation timeline >6 months | BU Head's planning horizon is quarterly |
| No industry-specific references | "You don't understand my business" |
| Pricing requires corporate procurement process | Signals overhead and loss of BU control |
| Product requires cross-BU data sharing | Political minefield; BU Head won't champion |
| Generic "digital transformation" pitch | Too vague; BU Head wants specific metric improvement |
| Vendor already deeply embedded in competing BU | Conflict of interest concern |

## §7 Organizational Context & Political Dynamics

### 7.1 BU Structure Models Across Chinese Companies

| Company | Structure | BU Head Title | Key Dynamic |
|---------|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| ByteDance | 6 BUs (抖音, TikTok, 火山引擎, 飞书, etc.) | 业务负责人/BU CEO | High autonomy; CEO sets strategy, BUs execute independently |
| Tencent | BGs (IEG, WXG, CSIG, PCG, CDG) | 事业群总裁 | Massive BGs with sub-structure; 工作室群 within IEG |
| Alibaba | Multiple groups (淘天, 云智能, 本地生活, 菜鸟, etc.) | 集团CEO/总裁 | Post-2023 split into independent groups; extreme autonomy |
| Hikvision | BGs (PBG, EBG, SMBG, 创新业务) | BG负责人 | Industry-vertical organized; each with own sales + R&D |
| Meituan | Business units (到店, 到家, 美团优选, etc.) | 事业部负责人 | Frequent restructuring; survival-of-fittest culture |
| Haier | 产业线 above thousands of 小微 | 产业线负责人 | 人单合一 model; extreme decentralization |
| JD | Divisions (京东零售, 京东物流, 京东健康, etc.) | CEO of division | Some divisions separately listed; true independence |

### 7.2 The CEO-BU Head Power Dynamic

```
CEO Power Levers:                    BU Head Counter-Moves:
─────────────────                    ──────────────────────
• Set/change BU boundaries           • Build indispensable revenue
• Allocate/withdraw headcount        • Retain key talent loyalty to BU
• Approve/deny budget increases      • Generate profit to self-fund
• Reorganize/merge/dissolve BU       • Create external dependencies
• Promote/demote BU Head             • Build external reputation
• Set BU performance targets         • Negotiate targets carefully
• Access to all BU data              • Information asymmetry in reporting
```

**Critical insight:** The BU Head's job security correlates directly with:
1. Revenue/profit delivery against targets
2. CEO's personal trust and confidence
3. Strategic importance of the BU's market
4. Replaceability — does anyone else know the business as well?

### 7.3 Cross-BU Dynamics

| Scenario | Typical Resolution |
|----------|-------------------|
| Two BUs targeting same customer | CEO designates "lead BU"; other provides support (reluctantly) |
| BU wants to enter another BU's territory | Must get CEO approval; often triggers reorganization |
| Shared technology/platform dispute | Group CTO mediates; BU Head with more revenue usually wins |
| Talent poaching between BUs | Officially banned; practically happens via "mutual transfer" fiction |
| Customer wants integrated cross-BU solution | 联合项目 (joint project) with one BU as lead; complex politics |

### 7.4 The Reorganization Cycle

Chinese tech companies reorganize frequently (Alibaba: 2–3 times/year at peak). BU Heads must be politically aware:

**Warning signs your BU is at risk:**
- CEO stops attending your QBR
- Headcount freeze while other BUs are hiring
- Another BU Head getting more public CEO endorsement
- Your BU's metrics plateau while competitors' BUs grow
- Group function leaders start "standardization" initiatives in your domain
- CEO asks about your "succession plan" or "next role ideas"

**Survival strategies:**
- Keep BU growth rate above average of peer BUs
- Maintain 1–2 "only I can do this" capabilities (key relationships, domain expertise)
- Build external reputation (industry visibility) as insurance
- Develop strong relationships with 2–3 board members beyond CEO
- Keep team morale high — a demoralized BU is easy to dissolve

### 9.4 Hardware/IoT BU Head

- Key challenge: Regulatory (especially US/EU), localization, dual-stack architecture
- Technology focus: Compliance tech, local market adaptation, data sovereignty
- Buying behavior: Needs local partners; higher willingness to buy vs. build internationally

## §11 Engagement Playbook for Sellers

### 11.1 Key Principles

1. **Sell to the BU, not the corporation** — The BU Head is your buyer; don't go through corporate procurement unless forced.

2. **Speed is everything** — If you can't show value in 30–90 days, you won't get a second chance.

3. **BU-specific value props** — Generic "enterprise" messaging fails. Know the BU's specific metrics, competitive position, and immediate challenges.

4. **Reference their specific horse race** — "Your competitors (both internal and external) are doing X" is the most powerful motivator.

5. **Respect their autonomy** — Never imply they need corporate approval for something within their authority.

6. **Land through the BU VP, expand through the BU Head** — Technical proof with the VP team; strategic expansion conversation with BU Head.

### 11.2 Messaging by Archetype

| Archetype | Opening Message | Value Proposition Frame |
|-----------|----------------|------------------------|
| 赛马冠军型 | "Help you extend your lead and make your BU indispensable" | Competitive advantage acceleration; metrics that impress CEO |
| 产业开拓型 | "Accelerate your path to product-market fit" | Speed to revenue; reduce time from concept to paying customers |
| 稳态经营型 | "Improve margins without disrupting what works" | Efficiency gains; cost optimization with zero downtime risk |
| 工作室群主 | "Give your creative teams superpowers" | Productivity tools that don't constrain creativity; successful case studies from peers |
| 平台赋能型 | "Make your platform the standard that other BUs can't live without" | Adoption metrics; developer experience; competitive benchmarks vs. external alternatives |
| 人单合一型 | "Help your micro-enterprises scale without adding overhead" | Automation, self-service capabilities, lightweight deployment |

### 11.3 Red Lines (Never Do This)

| Action | Why It Fails |
|--------|-------------|
| Go to corporate IT "first" then come to BU | BU Head sees you as corporate's tool, not their ally |
| Mention you're also working with a competing BU | Triggers territorial defense; deal dies |
| Require 6+ month implementation | Exceeds BU Head's planning patience |
| Ask for cross-BU data/access | Political minefield; instant rejection |
| Present a "corporate-wide" proposal | BU Head only cares about THEIR P&L |
| Name-drop the CEO without BU Head's knowledge | Violates trust; seen as "going over my head" |
| Miss a committed delivery date | Trust destruction is permanent with this persona |

## §13 Common Scenarios & Responses

### Scenario 1: CEO Announces Reorganization

**Situation:** CEO decides to merge your BU with an adjacent one; you may or may not lead the combined entity.

**BU Head Response:**
- Immediately assess: Am I likely to lead combined entity or lose my role?
- If likely to lead: Embrace; frame as "I've been preparing for this scale"
- If likely to lose: Negotiate hard — larger role elsewhere, or graceful exit with face saved
- Protect key team members regardless — their loyalty is your future capital

### Scenario 2: Another BU Encroaching on Your Territory

**Situation:** A peer BU starts building a product that overlaps with your core offering.

**BU Head Response:**
- Gather data showing your BU's superior position in this area
- Raise to CEO privately (never publicly) with framing: "This duplication wastes group resources"
- Simultaneously accelerate your own product to establish clear market leadership
- Offer a "collaboration proposal" that positions your BU as the lead with the other as contributor

### Scenario 3: Missing Quarterly Target

**Situation:** With 6 weeks left in Q, you're tracking 25% below revenue target.

**BU Head Response:**
- Identify 2–3 acceleration levers (accelerate deals, launch promotion, pull forward Q+1 revenue)
- Brief CEO proactively with recovery plan BEFORE the miss is public
- Frame as "temporary" and "already being addressed" — never frame as structural
- Sacrifice margin short-term if needed to hit revenue (then recover next Q)
- Never blame team publicly; take personal accountability to CEO

### Scenario 4: Key Talent Being Poached

**Situation:** Your VP Engineering received an offer from a competing BU Head (or external competitor) with 50% comp increase.

**BU Head Response:**
- Personal intervention within 24 hours — dinner, private conversation
- Counter-offer: comp increase + expanded scope + visible project ownership
- Involve CEO if the person is truly irreplaceable ("CEO personally values you")
- If you lose them: immediately backfill; never show organization-wide panic
- Long-term: build depth so no single departure is catastrophic

### Scenario 5: Vendor Presenting "Enterprise-Wide" Solution

**Situation:** A vendor approaches with a solution they want to sell to the entire corporation, starting with your BU.

**BU Head Response:**
- "我只关心我的BU的问题" (I only care about my BU's problems)
- If the solution genuinely helps: "Fine, let's do a BU-specific pilot. Don't involve other BUs."
- If vendor pushes corporate angle: "那你去找集团IT谈" (Then go talk to corporate IT) — deal killed
- BU Head will NEVER champion a cross-BU initiative that doesn't clearly benefit their BU first

## §14 Summary Decision Triggers

### 14.1 When the BU Head Says YES Fast

| Condition | Speed |
|-----------|-------|
